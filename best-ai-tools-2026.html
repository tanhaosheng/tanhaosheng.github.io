An "AI tool" is a billing line, not a category

The phrase "AI tool" hides more than it reveals. Grammarly and Runway both get the label, but one fixes your commas and the other generates video from text — different tasks, different skills, different failure modes. Treating them as one shelf of "AI stuff" is how buyers end up with nine subscriptions and no clear return. So this isn't a ranked list; it's a matrix: here's the task, here's the tool that fits, here's what it costs, and here's where it falls short.

The skeptic's read on "saves hours"

Every one of these advertises time saved. Some of that is real and measurable; some is the oldest software trick in the book — sell the gap between the demo and the daily grind. A few honest cuts:

  • Writing tools save time on the first draft and the cleanup, not on the thinking. If you didn't know what to say, the tool won't know either.
  • Coding assistants genuinely compress boilerplate and explanation. They do not compress the part where you decide what to build. The hours they save are real but bounded.
  • Scheduling/memory tools (Motion, Mem) save the most time for the most disorganized — and almost nothing for someone who already runs a tight system. Know which you are.
  • Image/video generators save hours of production only if the output is good enough to ship. Often it still needs a human finish, which quietly eats the savings back.

How to assemble a stack without eleven subscriptions

The freelancer's pragmatic bundle, by real price: Grammarly Free (writing), Canva Free (graphics), GitHub Copilot free-or-$10 if you code, ElevenLabs Free if you touch audio. That covers most knowledge-work needs for under $10–20/month. Add paid tiers only when a specific gap — brand memory, video generation, autonomous scheduling — actually costs you more in lost time than the subscription costs in cash.

The mistake is buying the category before you have the task. If nobody reads your blog, Jasper is a $39/month excuse. If you ship video weekly, Runway pays for itself. Match the tool to a recurring task you already have, not to an aspiration.

Frequently asked questions

Which of these are completely free?

Grammarly's basic checks, Canva's free tier, GitHub Copilot for students and OSS maintainers, ElevenLabs' free plan, and Whisper (if you run it locally) all cost $0. Most others have a free tier with real limits rather than a free product.

What's the best combination for a freelancer?

Grammarly Free + Canva Free + Copilot (free/student or $10) + ElevenLabs Free. Covers writing, graphics, code, and audio for under $20/month total. Add paid tiers only when a specific task justifies it.

Are AI writing tools safe to publish?

Safe to publish after you read and correct the output. Models invent facts and dates; treat every claim as a draft from a junior colleague. Search engines reward original, accurate writing regardless of the tool used to draft it.

Do I need separate tools for different tasks?

Not strictly — Canva now writes, Notion now does docs and projects. But specialists still win on their turf: Midjourney beats Canva on art; Copilot beats a chatbot on code. One or two generalists plus a few specialists is the sweet spot.

Verdict: There's no single "best AI tool" — only the tool that removes a task you already do. Buy by recurring task, not by category: writing polish → Grammarly, brand copy → Jasper, code → Copilot/Cursor, video → Runway, audio → ElevenLabs, notes → Notion AI, scheduling → Motion. The caveat: the time these save is real but bounded by the thinking they can't do for you — pay for the gap that actually costs you, and skip the rest.
