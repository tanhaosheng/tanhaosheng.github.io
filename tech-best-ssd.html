That "12,400 MB/s" number means less than you think

SSD marketing leads with one number: sequential read speed. The Crucial T700 advertises ~12,400 MB/s — more than double a Gen4 drive. Sounds like a clear win. It isn't, for most people. Sequential speed is what you feel only when moving huge files: 50 GB video renders, bulk backups, VM images. For boot times, app launches, and game loads, the number that matters is 4K random performance — and there, Gen4 and Gen5 are far closer. So before you pay the Gen5 premium, ask: do you actually move giant files, or do you just want a fast boot? The answer decides whether you're buying speed or a spec sheet.

What the spec sheet doesn't tell you

TLC vs QLC (and why all six here are TLC)

NAND type decides longevity and sustained speed. TLC (triple-level cell) is the mainstream sweet spot — fast and durable. QLC packs more bits per cell, cheaper, but slower under sustained writes and shorter-lived. Every drive in this table uses TLC. If you see a suspiciously cheap NVMe, check the NAND — it's probably QLC, and the price gap shows up under heavy writes.

DRAM cache vs HMB

A dedicated DRAM cache (all six here have one, except some budget HMB drives) keeps the mapping table fast, which helps sustained performance. DRAM-less drives using Host Memory Buffer are cheaper but can stutter under load. For an OS/boot drive, prefer a DRAM cache.

Sequential vs 4K random

Marketing shows sequential MB/s. Your daily experience is governed by 4K random IOPS — how fast the drive handles thousands of tiny files during boot and app launch. Gen4 drives already feel instant here; Gen5's massive sequential lead barely shows in everyday use. This is the core reason most buyers don't need Gen5.

Per drive: the honest read

  • Samsung 990 Pro — the balanced flagship: top Gen4 speed, mature firmware, strong sustained performance. The safe "best without surprises" pick.
  • WD Black SN850X — gaming-tuned, often priced below the 990 Pro, with optional heatsink. A strong value at the top end.
  • Crucial T700 — Gen5, ~2× Gen4 sequential throughput. Needs a PCIe 5.0 board, a heatsink, and airflow to sustain it. Buy only if you move huge files or do sustained I/O-heavy work.
  • Kingston KC3000 — near-flagship Gen4 at a lower price; the common "best value" recommendation.
  • Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus — enthusiast Gen4 with strong sustained speeds and flexible heatsink options.
  • Crucial MX500 — the SATA survivor: far slower, but relevant for older laptops without an M.2 slot or as cheap secondary storage.

PS5 storage expansion, specifically

The PS5 takes an NVMe M.2 PCIe 4.0 drive at ≥5,500 MB/s read, with a heatsink. That rules the MX500 (SATA) out entirely. The 990 Pro, SN850X, KC3000, and Rocket 4 Plus all qualify once a heatsink is added; the T700 (Gen5) also fits. If the goal is a PS5 upgrade, a Gen4 drive with a good heatsink is the right buy — Gen5's extra speed is wasted in that slot.

Frequently asked questions

NVMe or SATA?

NVMe (M.2) is far faster and similarly priced for new builds; choose SATA only if your machine lacks an M.2 slot or you need a cheap secondary drive. Check your laptop's slot type before buying.

Do I need a Gen5 SSD and a heatsink?

Gen5 delivers ~2× Gen4 sequential speed but requires a PCIe 5.0 board, a heatsink, and airflow to sustain it. For boot, gaming, and app loads, Gen4 already feels instant. Buy Gen5 only if you regularly do massive file transfers or I/O-heavy work.

How much capacity and endurance do I need?

1 TB is the current sweet spot for a boot + games/apps drive; 2 TB if you store large media. Modern drives carry generous TBW (600–1,180 here) and 5-year warranties, so for typical use capacity matters more than raw endurance headroom.

Why does my new SSD feel the same as the old one?

Because everyday speed is 4K random, not sequential. If your old drive was already NVMe Gen3/Gen4, a Gen4 or Gen5 upgrade rarely changes how fast apps open. The leap is only visible in large-file transfers.

Don't pay for Gen5 speed your daily workload will never touch — the spec sheet is not the same as the experience.
