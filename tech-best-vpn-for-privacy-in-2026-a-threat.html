A VPN doesn't make you anonymous

The first myth to retire: a VPN isn't a cloak, it's a handoff. Without it, your ISP sees your destination. With it, your VPN provider sees your destination instead. You've moved the witness, not erased them. So "best VPN for privacy" is really a question about which company you'd rather trust with your metadata — and under which laws they'd have to surrender it. That's the audit below.

The threat model, in plain terms

Before rating providers, name what you're defending against:

  • Passive surveillance — your ISP or Wi-Fi operator logging where you go. A VPN defeats this for everyone on the list.
  • Legal compulsion — a government ordering records. This is where jurisdiction decides everything.
  • Provider logging — the VPN itself keeping your activity. "No-logs" is a claim; an audit is evidence.
  • Technical leaks — DNS or WebRTC leaking your real IP even with the tunnel up.

Reading the audit

Jurisdiction is the quiet decider. Switzerland (Proton) and Panama (NordVPN) and the BVI (ExpressVPN) sit outside the 14-Eyes intelligence-sharing alliance. The Netherlands (Surfshark) is inside the 9-Eyes. None of this makes a provider "safe" — it changes who can legally compel them and what they'd be compelled to hand over. For a user whose threat model includes state surveillance, jurisdiction is the first filter, not the last.

Audits beat slogans. All four cite independent no-logs audits. Proton adds open-source clients, which lets outsiders inspect the code rather than trust a report. That's a meaningful step up for the privacy-purist tier of the threat model: reproducible trust beats a PDF.

Surfshark's edge is cost and devices. At the lowest entry price and with unlimited simultaneous connections, it's the rational pick for a household protecting many gadgets on a budget — provided you accept the Netherlands jurisdiction. For most users that trade is fine; for a targeted individual, it isn't.

Proton's edge is the free tier and the law. A genuinely usable free tier (one device, no data cap on its free plan) plus Swiss jurisdiction makes it the default recommendation when jurisdiction and cost both matter. The paid Plus tier adds Secure Core, routing through privacy-friendly countries first.

What no VPN does for you

  • It doesn't make you anonymous to the sites you visit. They still see your accounts, your browser fingerprint, your payment details. Tor, not a VPN, is for anonymity.
  • It doesn't legalize anything. A VPN changes who can see your traffic; it doesn't change the law.
  • It doesn't stop app-level leaks. If an app phones home over a channel that bypasses the tunnel, the VPN can't help. Kill Switch mitigates, doesn't eliminate.

Frequently asked questions

Which VPN is "most private"?

There's no single winner. If jurisdiction is your top concern, Proton (Switzerland) or NordVPN/ExpressVPN (outside 14-Eyes). If independent code review matters most, Proton's open-source apps lead. If budget and device count dominate, Surfshark. Match the pick to your threat model, not to a leaderboard.

Are the no-logs claims trustworthy?

All four cite independent audits, which is stronger than a marketing claim but not proof — an audit covers a point in time and a defined scope. Treat "audited no-logs" as meaningful evidence, not a guarantee. Proton's open-source clients let the technically inclined verify behavior directly.

Will a VPN stop my ISP seeing what I do?

Yes, for the destination of your traffic — the tunnel encrypts it between you and the VPN server. Your ISP sees you connected to a VPN, not which sites you opened. The VPN provider, however, sees the destinations instead. That's the handoff.

Do I still need to check for leaks myself?

Yes. Even audited clients can be misconfigured, and WebRTC in browsers can expose your real IP. Run a DNS and WebRTC leak test after connecting, and keep Kill Switch enabled. My audit can't see your setup; only your own check can.

Verdict: For maximum jurisdiction protection plus a usable free tier, Proton VPN (Switzerland, audited, open-source) is the privacy-purist default. For best balance of price, audit, and Panama jurisdiction, NordVPN. For unlimited devices on a tight budget, Surfshark — accept the Netherlands trade-off. The caveat: a VPN relocates your witness, it doesn't erase one. Audit your own leak settings, keep Kill Switch on, and remember no VPN makes you anonymous to the websites you log into.
