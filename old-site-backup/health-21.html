Understanding Diabetes: Types, Management, and Prevention

Diabetes is a chronic condition that affects how your body processes blood sugar. With proper management, people with diabetes can lead full, healthy lives.

Type 1 vs. Type 2 Diabetes

Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition where the pancreas produces little or no insulin. It typically appears in childhood and requires lifelong insulin therapy. Type 2 diabetes, more common in adults, involves insulin resistance and is often linked to lifestyle factors.

Risk Factors and Warning Signs

Risk factors for Type 2 diabetes include excess weight, physical inactivity, family history, and age. Symptoms include frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, blurred vision, and slow-healing wounds.

Blood Sugar Management

Monitoring blood glucose levels, taking medications as prescribed, and managing carbohydrate intake are essential. Regular physical activity improves insulin sensitivity. A consistent schedule for meals and medications helps maintain stable levels.

Dietary Recommendations

Focus on fiber-rich vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins, and healthy fats. Limit sugary beverages, refined carbohydrates, and processed foods. The glycemic index can guide food choices, but portion control remains the most important factor.

Preventing Type 2 Diabetes

Losing even 5 to 7 percent of body weight, exercising 150 minutes per week, and eating a balanced diet can reduce the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes by more than 50 percent. Regular screening is recommended for adults over 45 or those with risk factors.
