Cardio Exercise for Heart Health and Endurance

Cardiovascular exercise, also known as aerobic exercise, strengthens your heart and lungs while improving overall endurance. It is a cornerstone of any fitness routine.

Why Cardio Matters

Regular cardio reduces the risk of heart disease, lowers blood pressure, improves cholesterol levels, and helps manage weight. It also boosts mood through the release of endorphins and enhances sleep quality.

Types of Cardio Exercise

Walking, running, cycling, swimming, rowing, and dancing are all excellent forms of cardio. High-Intensity Interval Training alternates bursts of intense effort with recovery periods and offers efficient workouts in less time.

How Much Cardio Do You Need?

Health guidelines recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity per week. Spread this across at least three sessions for consistent benefits.

Getting Started Safely

Begin with low-impact activities like walking or cycling if you are new to exercise. Gradually increase duration and intensity. Warm up for five minutes before each session and cool down afterward to prevent injury.

Tracking Progress

Use a heart rate monitor, fitness tracker, or perceived exertion scale to gauge intensity. Aim for 50 to 85 percent of your maximum heart rate during workouts. Increase distance or time by no more than 10 percent per week.
