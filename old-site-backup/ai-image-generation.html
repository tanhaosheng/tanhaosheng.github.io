AI Image Generation Guide 2026: Tools, Tips, and How to Get Started

Creating stunning images with artificial intelligence is now accessible to everyone. Whether you are a designer, marketer, or hobbyist, this guide will help you navigate the best tools and techniques.

AI Image Generation Has Gone Mainstream

Not long ago, generating a realistic image from text felt like science fiction. Today, anyone with an internet connection can produce professional-quality visuals in seconds. AI image generation has matured rapidly, and 2026 offers more choice than ever.

The landscape now includes four major players: Midjourney, DALL-E 3, Adobe Firefly, and Stable Diffusion. Each has distinct strengths, pricing models, and ideal use cases. Choosing the right one depends on your goals, budget, and technical comfort level.

This guide covers how each tool works, step-by-step tutorials for getting started, techniques for writing better image prompts, and important ethical considerations you should know.

How AI Image Generation Works

At a high level, AI image generators use a type of model called a diffusion model. The model is trained on billions of images and their text descriptions. When you type a prompt, the model starts with random noise and gradually refines it into an image that matches your description.

You do not need to understand the technical details to use these tools well. But knowing the basics helps: the model has never seen your image before. It generates a completely new one based on patterns it learned during training. This is why results can be surprising, creative, and sometimes imperfect.

Tool 1: Midjourney

Midjourney remains the gold standard for artistic and stylized imagery. It operates through Discord, which gives it a unique community-driven feel. You type prompts in a Discord channel and the bot returns four generated images to choose from.

How to Use Midjourney

  1. Join Discord and the Midjourney server. Go to midjourney.com and click "Join the Beta." Create a Discord account if you do not have one.
  2. Subscribe to a plan. Midjourney requires a paid subscription. Plans start at around $10 per month for basic usage and go up to $60 for unlimited generation and commercial rights.
  3. Find a newbie room. In the Midjourney server, go to one of the #newbies channels. These are shared rooms where you can test prompts.
  4. Type /imagine followed by your prompt. For example: /imagine a serene mountain lake at sunrise, cinematic lighting, 4k. The bot will process your request and return four images within about 60 seconds.
  5. Upscale or vary your results. Below each result set, buttons let you upscale an image (U1-U4) or create variations (V1-V4). Experiment with different options to refine your output.

Best for: Artists, game designers, creative professionals, and anyone who wants highly stylized, cinematic imagery. Midjourney excels at fantasy, sci-fi, portraiture, and conceptual art.

Limitations: Requires Discord. No native web interface. The learning curve for prompts is steeper than other tools. Text rendering in images is poor.

Tool 2: DALL-E 3 in ChatGPT

DALL-E 3, integrated directly into ChatGPT, is the most accessible AI image generator. If you already use ChatGPT, you can generate images without leaving the chat interface. Just describe what you want in natural language.

How to Use DALL-E 3

  1. Open ChatGPT. You need a ChatGPT Plus subscription ($20 per month) or a ChatGPT Pro plan to access DALL-E 3. Free tier users do not have image generation access.
  2. Describe your image in plain language. Type something like: "Create an illustration of a cozy bookstore cafe with warm lighting, bookshelves covering the walls, and a cat sleeping on a window seat."
  3. Review and refine. DALL-E 3 will generate one image per request. If it is not quite right, tell ChatGPT what to change. "Make the lighting warmer" or "Add more plants" works naturally.
  4. Download your image. Click on the generated image to open it, then right-click and save. The image resolution is 1024x1024 by default.

Best for: Casual users, writers who need quick illustrations, social media content creators, and anyone who wants a simple text-to-image experience without learning a new platform.

Limitations: Lower resolution than Midjourney. Less artistic control. Only one image per generation. No batch processing. Cannot generate photorealistic faces as accurately as some competitors.

Tool 3: Adobe Firefly

Adobe Firefly stands out for one critical reason: commercial safety. Firefly is trained on licensed content and public domain images, which means the images you generate are safe to use in commercial projects without copyright concerns.

How to Use Adobe Firefly

  1. Go to firefly.adobe.com. You can start with a free tier that gives you a limited number of generative credits each month.
  2. Choose your mode. Firefly offers several modes: Text to Image, Generative Fill, Text Effects, and more. Start with Text to Image.
  3. Write your prompt. Use the style selector to pick a visual style (photographic, graphic, fantasy, etc.). Then type your prompt and adjust settings like aspect ratio and intensity.
  4. Generate and edit. Firefly produces four variations. You can then use Generative Fill to edit specific areas of the image, or use the selection tools to replace objects.
  5. Export with confidence. Firefly images include Content Credentials, a digital label that verifies they were generated by AI. Download in PNG or JPEG format.

Best for: Graphic designers, marketers, and businesses that need commercially safe images. Firefly integrates with Photoshop, Illustrator, and Express, making it a natural choice for Adobe users.

Limitations: Fewer creative credits on the free plan. Less artistic variety than Midjourney. Some styles can feel generic compared to competitors.

Tool 4: Stable Diffusion

Stable Diffusion is the open-source option. It is free, runs locally on your own computer, and offers the most technical control. For users who want full control over every parameter, or who need to generate images at scale, Stable Diffusion is unmatched.

How to Use Stable Diffusion

  1. Choose an interface. The easiest way to start is with Automatic1111's Web UI or ComfyUI. These are community-built interfaces that run in your browser.
  2. Install the software. You will need a computer with a dedicated GPU (NVIDIA recommended with at least 6GB VRAM). Download the installer from the official GitHub repository and follow the setup guide.
  3. Download a model. Stable Diffusion uses model checkpoints. The base SDXL model is a good starting point. You can also download specialized models trained for specific styles, like anime or photorealism.
  4. Write your prompt and generate. Type a prompt, adjust settings like CFG scale (how closely the image follows your prompt), sampling steps, and image size. Click Generate.
  5. Experiment with advanced features. Stable Diffusion supports inpainting (editing specific areas), outpainting (extending images beyond their borders), ControlNet (using a reference image to guide generation), and LoRAs (small model add-ons for specific styles).

Best for: Developers, technical users, privacy-conscious users (everything runs locally), and anyone who needs unlimited free generation or wants to fine-tune models on their own data.

Limitations: Steep learning curve. Requires a powerful computer. No official support or customer service. Interface quality varies by community build.

How to Write Better Image Prompts

The same principles of prompt engineering apply to image generation, with a few twists. Image prompts work best when you describe what you see, not what you want the AI to think.

Here are the key elements of a strong image prompt:

  • Subject: What is the main focus? "A Siberian husky," "a vintage motorcycle," "a futuristic city skyline."
  • Medium and Style: What should it look like? "Oil painting," "photorealistic," "anime style," "cinematic," "watercolor," "3D render."
  • Lighting and Color: "Golden hour lighting," "neon blue and pink," "soft diffused light," "dramatic shadows."
  • Composition: "Close-up shot," "wide angle," "aerial view," "symmetrical composition," "shallow depth of field."
  • Mood and Atmosphere: "Mysterious," "cozy," "melancholic," "energetic," "serene."
❌ "A cat"

✅ "A fluffy orange cat sitting on a windowsill, afternoon sunlight streaming in, photorealistic, shallow depth of field, warm tones, 4k"
❌ "Robot"

✅ "A steampunk robot with brass gears and copper plating, standing in a Victorian-era workshop, cinematic lighting, volumetric fog, intricate details, 8k"
Tip: Many image generators respond well to "quality boosters" at the end of your prompt. Words like "4k," "8k," "highly detailed," "intricate," "masterpiece," and "trending on ArtStation" can improve output quality significantly.

Ethical Considerations and Copyright

AI image generation raises important ethical and legal questions that every user should understand:

  • Copyright ownership: In most jurisdictions, AI-generated images are not copyrightable because they lack human authorship. This means anyone can use, modify, and distribute them. Some platforms grant you commercial usage rights through their terms of service. Always read the fine print.
  • Training data concerns: Many models were trained on images scraped from the internet without artists' consent. This has led to lawsuits and ongoing debates. If ethical sourcing matters to you, choose Adobe Firefly, which trains only on licensed content.
  • Deepfakes and misinformation: AI image generators can create convincing images of real people, places, and events. Never use these tools to create deceptive content. Many platforms now require Content Credentials or watermarks to indicate AI generation.
  • Artist displacement: The rise of AI art has real economic consequences for illustrators, photographers, and designers. Consider using AI generation as a complementary tool rather than a complete replacement for human artists.
Important: Always check the terms of service for the platform you use. Some prohibit generating images of public figures, brand logos, or copyrighted characters. Respect these rules to avoid account suspension or legal issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which AI image generator is completely free?

Stable Diffusion is completely free and open source, but requires a computer with a good GPU. Adobe Firefly offers a free tier with limited monthly credits. Bing Image Creator (powered by DALL-E) offers free generation with some limitations. No major tool offers unlimited free high-resolution generation.

Can I use AI-generated images for my business?

Yes, but check the license. Midjourney's paid plans grant commercial rights. Adobe Firefly is explicitly designed for commercial use. DALL-E 3 via ChatGPT Plus allows commercial use. Stable Diffusion's license varies by model checkpoint. When in doubt, use Adobe Firefly for the safest commercial coverage.

How do I get consistent characters across multiple images?

Midjourney has a "Consistent Character" feature using reference images. With Stable Diffusion, you can use a LoRA trained on your character. DALL-E 3 is less reliable for consistency. The most reliable approach is to generate one image you like and use it as a reference for subsequent prompts.

Why do AI images sometimes have weird hands and fingers?

Hands are notoriously difficult for diffusion models because they have complex structure and appear in many configurations. Most tools have improved significantly, but you may still get extra fingers or unnatural poses. Specify "perfect hands" or "natural hand pose" in your prompt, or use inpainting to fix problem areas.

What is the best tool for photorealistic images?

Midjourney produces the most consistently photorealistic results, especially with version 6 and later. Stable Diffusion with a photorealistic fine-tuned model (like Realistic Vision) can match or exceed Midjourney with the right settings. DALL-E 3 and Firefly are capable but generally less realistic.

How can I remove the background from an AI-generated image?

Adobe Firefly has a built-in background removal tool. Midjourney does not offer direct background removal, but you can use third-party tools like remove.bg. Stable Diffusion supports inpainting, which lets you manually mask and replace backgrounds.
