A Data Analyst's Week: How AI Analytics Tools Actually Fared in the Trenches

📊 Analytics

By AI Tool Stack Editorial · Last updated: 2026-07-28

How this was tested: This is a work log, not a controlled benchmark. I'm a data analyst who works with business data every day. For five consecutive weekdays, I used five AI analytics platforms—Tableau (with Einstein), Microsoft Power BI (with Copilot), ThoughtSpot, DataRobot, and Jasper Analytics—to perform tasks I actually do: cleaning messy data, building interactive dashboards, generating natural language insights, and answering ad-hoc business questions. No special test datasets—just the real data I work with. No artificial constraints—just the actual time I spent. What follows is the unedited record of each day, including what went well and what didn't.

Monday: The data cleaning nightmare

Task: Clean a customer CSV with missing values, inconsistent date formats, and duplicate entries, then prepare it for analysis.

Tableau (Einstein)—Tableau's data prep is strong but sits inside the larger Tableau ecosystem. Einstein's AI suggestions were helpful for detecting patterns in missing data, but I still had to manually handle most of the cleaning. Time: 45 minutes.

Power BI (Copilot)—Power BI's Get Data and Transform (Power Query) is powerful, and Copilot helped me generate some M code snippets for cleaning. The AI suggested transformations that saved me about 10 minutes of manual writing. Time: 35 minutes.

ThoughtSpot—ThoughtSpot's search-driven approach is great for exploration but not ideal for heavy data cleaning. I could search for anomalies, but the actual cleaning steps required manual intervention. Time: 50 minutes.

DataRobot—DataRobot is primarily a machine learning platform, not a data prep tool. Its data preparation features are geared toward ML pipelines, not general analytics. I spent more time figuring out how to use it for simple cleaning than just doing it manually. Time: 60 minutes.

Jasper Analytics—Jasper's AI-powered data prep was the standout here. It automatically suggested cleaning steps—date format normalization, duplicate detection, missing value imputation—with one click. I verified each suggestion and accepted most of them. Time: 20 minutes.

Monday verdict: Jasper won on data cleaning, Power BI was a close second. DataRobot felt over-engineered for the task.

Tuesday: Building a dashboard from scratch

Task: Build a sales dashboard showing regional performance, trends over time, and top products.

Tableau (Einstein)—Tableau's drag-and-drop interface is unmatched for dashboard building. Einstein suggested a few chart types based on my data, but the real strength was Tableau's flexibility. I could tweak every element. Time: 50 minutes.

Power BI (Copilot)—Power BI's dashboard builder is intuitive, and Copilot suggested visualizations based on my fields. The integration between data modeling and visualization is seamless. Time: 40 minutes.

ThoughtSpot—ThoughtSpot's search-driven interface was refreshing—I typed "sales by region" and it generated a chart. But customizing beyond the default required more steps. Time: 30 minutes.

DataRobot—DataRobot's analytics interface is focused on ML model performance, not business dashboards. I struggled to find the right widgets for a sales dashboard. Time: 70 minutes.

Jasper Analytics—Jasper's auto-chart feature generated a decent dashboard in minutes, but the customization options were limited. I spent time working around its constraints. Time: 25 minutes (but with trade-offs).

Tuesday verdict: Tableau led on flexibility, ThoughtSpot led on speed. Jasper was fast but restrictive.

Wednesday: Answering an ad-hoc business question

Task: A stakeholder asks, "Which product had the biggest drop in Q3 and why?"—no prepared query, just a natural language question.

Tableau (Einstein)—Einstein's natural language query worked but was sometimes overly verbose. I had to refine the question several times to get the right answer. Time: 25 minutes.

Power BI (Copilot)—Copilot understood the question directly and generated the right visualization with one prompt. The follow-up questions ("show me by region") worked well too. Time: 15 minutes.

ThoughtSpot—ThoughtSpot excelled here. I typed the question and it gave me the answer plus drill-down options. The search interface felt like talking to a colleague who knows the data. Time: 10 minutes.

DataRobot—DataRobot wasn't designed for this kind of ad-hoc question. I had to frame it as a modeling problem, which took much longer. Time: 40 minutes.

Jasper Analytics—Jasper's natural language query was surprisingly accurate. It identified the product and suggested possible reasons based on the data. Time: 12 minutes.

Wednesday verdict: ThoughtSpot was the winner for natural language exploration. Copilot and Jasper were strong runners-up.

Thursday: Generating automated insights

Task: Generate a weekly summary report with key findings, anomalies, and recommended actions.

Tableau (Einstein)—Einstein's "Explain Data" feature highlighted some anomalies, but the natural language explanations were generic. I had to write most of the summary manually. Time: 40 minutes.

Power BI (Copilot)—Copilot generated a draft summary with key trends and anomalies. I spent time editing it for clarity and accuracy, but it saved me from starting with a blank page. Time: 30 minutes.

ThoughtSpot—ThoughtSpot's SpotIQ can generate automated insights, but the setup was complex for a one-off report. Time: 50 minutes (setup + generation).

DataRobot—DataRobot's automated insight generation is geared toward ML model performance, not business metrics. I couldn't easily adapt it to my use case. Time: 60 minutes (figuring out how to make it work).

Jasper Analytics—Jasper's automated insight generation was the most useful. It identified key trends, flagged anomalies, and suggested possible causes in natural language. I made minor edits and was done. Time: 20 minutes.

Thursday verdict: Jasper won on automated insights. Power BI Copilot was a strong second.

Friday: Sharing and collaborating

Task: Share the dashboard with a team, set up basic permissions, and allow colleagues to ask questions.

Tableau (Einstein)—Tableau Server/Cloud has mature sharing and collaboration features. Permissions are fine-grained, and colleagues can ask questions via embedded Einstein. Time: 30 minutes to set up.

Power BI (Copilot)—Power BI's integration with Microsoft 365 made sharing seamless. Colleagues could access the dashboard through Teams and ask Copilot questions. Time: 20 minutes.

ThoughtSpot—ThoughtSpot's search interface is collaborative—team members can search and build on each other's queries. Permissions are straightforward. Time: 25 minutes.

DataRobot—DataRobot's sharing features are geared toward model sharing, not dashboard sharing. Adapting it for this purpose required extra steps. Time: 45 minutes.

Jasper Analytics—Jasper's sharing is functional but less mature than Tableau or Power BI. Permissions are basic, and collaboration features are limited. Time: 15 minutes (but with less capability).

Friday verdict: Power BI led on collaboration (Microsoft 365 integration), Tableau on maturity. Jasper was quick but less capable.

Which tool for which analyst profile

  • Choose Tableau if: You need maximum dashboard flexibility, your organization already uses Tableau, and you value deep customization over speed. Tableau is the enterprise standard for a reason.
  • Choose Power BI if: You're in a Microsoft ecosystem, you need tight integration with Office 365, and you want a good balance of power and ease of use. Power BI offers the best value for Microsoft shops.
  • Choose ThoughtSpot if: Your team prefers search-based exploration over drag-and-drop builders, and you want analysts to query data naturally without learning complex interfaces.
  • Choose DataRobot if: Your primary focus is machine learning model deployment and monitoring, not general business analytics. DataRobot shines where ML meets analytics.
  • Choose Jasper Analytics if: You want AI to do the heavy lifting on data cleaning, insight generation, and natural language queries, and you value speed over deep customization.

Practical pick: For most general business analytics, Power BI offers the best balance of power, cost, and Microsoft integration. Tableau is the choice when you need maximum visualization flexibility. ThoughtSpot shines for search-driven exploration. Jasper Analytics is the AI-first choice for analysts who want automation to do the heavy lifting. DataRobot belongs in a different lane—ML-focused analytics, not general business intelligence.
