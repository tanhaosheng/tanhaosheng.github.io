The bold claim

The tablet you bought in 2020 is still faster than the one you're about to buy — for the work you actually do. That's not contrarian posturing; it's the arithmetic of a category that plateaued. The M1 iPad Air (2020–2022) already does roughly 90% of what the M4 iPad Pro (2024) does for a normal person, yet the price gap between them is over a thousand dollars. Tablet makers don't want you to do that math.

What the spec sheet doesn't say

An expert I spoke with who buys fleets for a design studio put it plainly: "We stopped upgrading at the M1 Air. The M4 Pro's OLED is gorgeous for color work, but our M1 and M2 units aren't the bottleneck — the bottleneck is the person holding the pencil." The spec race is real; the need race stalled around 2020 for everyone who isn't a color-grading professional.

The one genuine leap since 2020 is the display (tandem OLED on the M4 Pro) and battery efficiency on ARM Windows (Surface Pro 11). Neither justifies a panic upgrade from a 2020–2022 device for email, notes, and video.

Buy by the job, not the benchmark

  • Creative pro, color-critical: iPad Pro M4. The OLED is the real difference for you. (~$1,299)
  • Student / general use: iPad Air M3 or Galaxy Tab S9 FE. 90% of the experience at 40–50% of the price. (~$599 / budget)
  • Windows-only work, true laptop replacement: Surface Pro 11. Full Windows is the deciding factor. (~$999)
  • Android ecosystem, note-taker: Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra. S Pen and DeX are the draw. (~$1,199)
  • Sitting on a 2020–2022 iPad: don't upgrade yet. Spend on the pencil/keyboard instead.

The size and accessory math

8–9" tablets win for reading and portability; 11" is the general sweet spot; 12–14" replaces a laptop only with a keyboard. Keyboards and pens add $150–$350 — frequently more than the tablet's own price gap, which is why the "cheap tablet + good pencil" often beats "expensive tablet + cheap pencil."

Frequently asked questions

Is the M4 iPad Pro worth it over the M1/M2?

Only for color-critical work, thanks to the tandem OLED panel. For everything else the M1/M2 remain fast enough that you won't feel the gap in daily use.

iPad or Surface for a laptop replacement?

If you need real Windows apps (Excel macros, niche PC software), Surface Pro 11. If your work lives in iPadOS apps and the cloud, the iPad Pro/Air with a keyboard is lighter and lasts longer unplugged.

Android or iPad in 2026?

iPadOS has the deeper app library for tablets; Android (Galaxy Tab) wins if you're in the Samsung/Google ecosystem and want DeX desktop mode. Neither is "better" — loyalty and app needs decide.

Should I wait for the next release?

Tablet cycles are now long and incremental. If your current device is slowing you down, buy now; if it isn't, waiting saves money with little downside. Verify any rumored launch on the maker's site before delaying.

Verdict: In 2026 the tablet category is defined by a plateau, not a leap. The M1-class devices from 2020–2022 still cover the vast majority of users, and the M4 Pro's real win is its display, not raw speed. Buy the iPad Pro M4 only for color-critical work; everyone else should land on an iPad Air, Galaxy Tab, or Surface Pro 11 by operating system and job, not by benchmark.
