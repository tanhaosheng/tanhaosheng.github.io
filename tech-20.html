From 11 Mbps to 40 Gbps — and why the number lies

In 1999, 802.11b gave you 11 Mbps if you were lucky and close. Then came g, n (WiFi 4), ac (WiFi 5), ax (WiFi 6/6E), and now be (WiFi 7), whose headline rate is "over 40 Gbps." That progression is real. The lie isn't the number — it's the implication that buying the newest router makes your Netflix faster. It doesn't, unless something upstream was already the bottleneck. So let's put the standards on a bench and compare what actually differs.

What the spec actually buys you

  • WiFi 6E's win is the 6 GHz band — a cleaner slice of spectrum away from crowded 2.4/5 GHz. If your apartment is a sea of neighbor signals, 6E alone removes most of the pain.
  • WiFi 7's wins are Multi-Link Operation (a device uses 5 + 6 GHz at once for steadier throughput) and 320 MHz channels. Meaningful for multi-gig LAN transfers and congested homes — not for a 300 Mbps internet plan.
  • The bottleneck is usually not the router. Public testing consistently shows that with a sub-gigabit ISP plan, WiFi 6E already saturates the connection. WiFi 7 mostly helps inside your network (NAS transfers, local streaming) and future-proofs.

Single router vs mesh

Under ~1,500 sq ft with decent central placement, one good router is fine. Larger or multi-floor homes get dead spots a single radio can't reach; mesh (Eero, TP-Link Deco, Netgear Orbi) uses nodes that hand off seamlessly. The cost is price and a second/third power outlet — not performance, if wired backhaul is an option.

A config example (because "set it and forget it" bites)

Most slowdown complaints trace to auto-channel chaos. Pinning 5 GHz to a low-interference channel is a classic fix — here's the shape of it on an OpenWrt-style setup:

# Pin 5 GHz to a clean 80 MHz channel and commit
uci set wireless.radio5g.channel='36'
uci set wireless.radio5g.htmode='HE80'
uci set wireless.radio5g.country='US'
uci commit wireless
wifi reload

That's not magic — it just stops the router from flapping between congested channels. Your firmware's UI does the same thing with dropdowns.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need WiFi 7 in 2026?

Only if you have multi-gig internet or do large local transfers (NAS, 4K editing over the network) and own devices that support it. For most sub-gigabit homes, WiFi 6E is the better value; WiFi 7 is forward-looking.

Will a new router speed up my internet?

Only if your old router was the bottleneck. If a speed test at the modem already shows your full plan rate, a new router changes little. Test at the modem first.

Mesh or a single router?

Single router for small, open homes; mesh for larger or multi-floor spaces with dead spots. Wired backhaul between nodes beats wireless backhaul when you can run a cable.

What about security?

Use WPA3, disable WPS, set a strong admin password, enable auto firmware updates, and put IoT gadgets on a separate SSID/VLAN where your router supports it. The radio standard doesn't secure you — the configuration does.
