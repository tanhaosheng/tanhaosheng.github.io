Understanding Allergies: Causes, Symptoms, and Relief

Allergies occur when the immune system overreacts to harmless substances. They affect millions of people and range from mild seasonal nuisances to life-threatening emergencies.

Common Types of Allergies

Seasonal allergies are triggered by pollen from trees, grasses, and weeds. Food allergies commonly involve peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, eggs, and dairy. Other types include dust mites, pet dander, mold, insect stings, and medication allergies.

Symptoms and Severity

Symptoms range from sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes, and skin rashes to more severe reactions like swelling, difficulty breathing, and anaphylaxis. Symptoms can appear immediately or hours after exposure depending on the allergy type.

Medical Treatments

Antihistamines, decongestants, nasal corticosteroids, and eye drops provide symptom relief. Allergy immunotherapy, available as shots or sublingual tablets, gradually desensitizes the immune system. Severe allergies require carrying an epinephrine auto-injector.

Natural Management Strategies

Reduce exposure by keeping windows closed during high pollen seasons, using air purifiers with HEPA filters, and washing bedding in hot water. Saline nasal rinses flush out allergens. Local honey is not scientifically proven but many people report benefit.

When to See an Allergist

If over-the-counter medications are not providing relief, or if you experience severe reactions, consult an allergist. Skin prick tests or blood tests can identify specific triggers, allowing for targeted treatment and avoidance strategies.
