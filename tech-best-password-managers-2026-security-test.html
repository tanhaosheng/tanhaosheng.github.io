3 a.m., a password reset email you didn't request

You didn't click anything. But an email just arrived: "Your password was changed." Someone tried your reused password from a breach you forgot about on a forum you forgot you joined. This is the scene every password-manager pitch evokes — and it's real enough. Credential reuse is how most account takeovers start. The tool fixes that. The question is which tool, and whether you need to pay for it at all.

The contrarian pick: you probably don't need to pay

The affiliate-driven "best password manager" lists almost all crown a paid app. The honest, less lucrative answer: for the large majority of people, a free, open-source, audited manager already does the only thing that matters — generates unique passwords and encrypts them locally under a key only you hold. Bitwarden's free tier and KeePass both clear that bar. Paying buys convenience (family sharing, travel mode, polished apps), not security you lack.

What each one is genuinely for

  • Bitwarden is the default recommendation: open source, independently audited, free across unlimited devices, and cheap to upgrade. The only knock is that the mobile app feels plainer than 1Password's.
  • KeePass is for people who want their vault in a local file they control completely — no server, no company. The cost is setup friction; there's no official mobile app, so you rely on community ports.
  • 1Password earns its subscription with the smoothest UX, Watchtower monitoring, and Travel Mode. If a household or non-technical family needs "just works," the ~$3/mo is fair.
  • Dashlane / NordPass add VPN or bundle perks; fine if you want them, but they don't out-secure Bitwarden on the core job.
  • LastPass recovered from a 2022 breach but carries that history; its free tier is now single-device, which limits the core use case.

The pricing math that matters

If you manage one person's passwords, $0 (Bitwarden free or KeePass) is the right number. The moment you need family sharing or want polished cross-device apps, ~$10–40/year is the realistic spend. Anything much above that should come with a feature you'll actually use, not a badge.

Frequently asked questions

Is a free manager actually safe?

Bitwarden's free tier uses the same zero-knowledge encryption as its paid plans and has been independently audited. "Free" here means the business model isn't the vault — it's the premium and team tiers. Safety of the core function isn't gated behind payment.

What's the one feature worth paying for?

For most, nothing — the free tier covers unique passwords and syncing. Family sharing and a polished app are the common reasons people upgrade. Don't pay for "more security"; pay for convenience you'll use.

Can I self-host instead?

Bitwarden's server is open source (vaultwarden is a popular lightweight reimplementation), and KeePass is fully local. Self-hosting removes the vendor entirely but adds backup responsibility — you become the ops team.

Should I trust cloud sync with my passwords?

Zero-knowledge architecture means the provider stores encrypted blobs it can't read; your master password is the only key. The real risk is a weak master password or reused one. Use a long passphrase and enable 2FA on the manager itself.
