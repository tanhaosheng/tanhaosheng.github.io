The bill that hides on page nine

Most Azure intros talk about regions and compliance. Let's start where the pain actually is: egress. Getting your data out of Azure costs roughly $0.08–$0.12 per GB beyond the first 100 GB/month, depending on region (azure.com/bandwidth). That sounds tiny until you move a terabyte: 1,000 GB × ~$0.09 ≈ $90 to extract data you already paid to store. Multiply by a monthly pipeline and a "cheap" storage tier becomes a toll road. No cloud vendor leads with this number, which is exactly why it should lead the conversation.

Myth-busting the obvious takes

Myth 1: "Azure is only for big enterprises." False. Azure owns GitHub, and its developer story (VS Code, GitHub Actions, Azure DevOps) is genuinely strong for small teams. The enterprise reputation is real, but the dev tooling isn't bad — it's just overshadowed by the sales motion.

Myth 2: "AWS is always cheapest." Also false. AWS's base compute can look cheap, then egress, NAT gateway, per-request Lambda, and support tiers stack up. Independent cost comparisons repeatedly show the winner flips by workload. Cheap compute is not a cheap bill.

Myth 3: "Azure lags on AI." Azure OpenAI Service gives enterprise-grade access to OpenAI models with the compliance story enterprises want — that's why many Fortune 500 AI deployments run on Azure, not directly on OpenAI. It's less of a consumer headline, more of an enterprise lock-in win.

What the benchmarks actually say

Public cloud comparisons don't produce a single "winner" because there's no one benchmark. What's verifiable: Azure and AWS both publish 60-ish/30-ish regions; independent VM benchmarks (e.g., Phoronix-style runs) show parity on commodity compute and differences only on tuned, workload-specific instances. Translation for buyers: don't pick on raw speed — pick on the ecosystem you already live in. The performance gap is smaller than the migration cost of switching later.

Where analysts land

The major analyst frameworks (Gartner's Cloud Infrastructure Magic Quadrant, for instance) consistently place Azure and AWS as leaders with GCP close behind on vision and data. The analysts' public commentary converges on the same editor's take above: match the cloud to your existing stack, because the three are converging on features and diverging mainly in ecosystem and price structure. We're paraphrasing their published positioning, not quoting — the reports are paywalled.

Choose by your reality

  • You're a Microsoft shop (M365, AD, .NET): Azure. The integration is the whole point; fighting it to save $20/mo is false economy.
  • You're a startup wanting max flexibility: AWS still has the deepest catalog and the largest Stack Overflow history to google.
  • You're data/ML or Kubernetes-native: GCP's GKE and data services are the cleanest.
  • You run on-prem plus cloud: Azure Arc's single control plane is the strongest hybrid story of the three.

Frequently asked questions

Is Azure more expensive than AWS?

Not systematically. List prices are comparable; the difference shows up in egress, support tiers, and which services you lean on. Model your actual workload — don't trust a homepage calculator that omits bandwidth.

Can I use Azure without a Microsoft background?

Yes. The Azure CLI, REST APIs, and GitHub integration work fine for non-Microsoft stacks. You'll just miss the AD/M365 synergies that make it shine for everyone else.

What's the real risk with Azure?

Two: egress lock-in (getting data out costs money) and the inconsistent portal/docs experience that slows new teams. Neither is fatal; both are worth knowing before you commit.

Is Azure good for AI?

For enterprise AI, yes — Azure OpenAI Service wraps OpenAI models with compliance and isolation many regulated buyers require. For bleeding-edge research, the model labs themselves may move faster, but Azure is where enterprises deploy.

Verdict: Azure isn't "the enterprise cloud" — it's the Microsoft-shop cloud, and that's a stronger recommendation than the marketing admits. Pick it if you live in M365, AD, or .NET, or need the best hybrid control plane via Arc. Don't pick it on a homepage price; the egress line is where the real cost hides. The editor's one-line take: match the cloud to the stack you already own, because the three are closer in power than in billing.
