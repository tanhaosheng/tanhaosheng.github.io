Everyone asks "which is best." That's the wrong question.

"Best" implies a podium — one winner, everyone else runner-up. Chinese AI tools don't work like that. They're a toolbox. DeepSeek is a scalpel, Qwen is a Swiss army knife, Kimi is a filing cabinet with a brain, Doubao is a chatty companion, ERNIE is the cautious librarian. The one you reach for depends on the job already in your hand. So let's meet them as tools, not contestants — and then I'll make an unpopular case for which one a builder should actually default to.

What each one is like to use

DeepSeek is the name everyone knows, and fairly. Its R1 reasoning model does genuine chain-of-thought work, and the API price undercuts US frontierers by an order of magnitude. For a solo developer or a cash-strapped startup, it's the obvious first stop.

Qwen is the quiet workhorse. Alibaba ships it in sizes from under a billion parameters to over a hundred billion, with vision variants (Qwen-VL) that can read charts and photos. Because the weights are Apache-2.0, you can download, fine-tune, and self-host. It rarely tops a "most impressive demo" list — and that's exactly why builders underrate it.

Kimi was built for one job: long context. Throw a 200-page PDF at it and ask questions about page 140. For legal, academic, or technical document work, that focus shows.

Doubao and ERNIE are stronger inside China's consumer and enterprise ecosystems than they are as global developer defaults. Worth knowing, less often the right pick for an English-speaking builder.

The contrarian pick: default to Qwen, not DeepSeek

Here's the unpopular take. The community reflex is "just use DeepSeek, it's free and it's the best." But if you're building a product — not chatting — Qwen is the smarter default, and most people only realize it after they hit a wall.

  • One model can't do everything. DeepSeek is text-and-reasoning first. The moment you need vision (reading a screenshot, a chart, a scanned form), you're reaching for another provider. Qwen gives you text and VL in one family, so your integration stays in one place.
  • Size matters for cost. Qwen ships tiny models you can run on a laptop and huge ones for the hard jobs. DeepSeek's small options are thinner. For edge or on-device use, Qwen's range is hard to beat.
  • The weights are truly open. Both are open, but Qwen's Apache-2.0 license and breadth of sizes make self-hosting and fine-tuning markedly easier to plan around.

None of this dims DeepSeek's star. It's still the reasoning pick. But "best Chinese AI tool" for a builder is Qwen — the one everyone lists second.

Frequently asked questions

Are Chinese AI tools safe for sensitive work?

It depends on your threat model and jurisdiction. Open-weight models like Qwen and DeepSeek can be self-hosted so data never leaves your infrastructure. Hosted APIs are subject to the vendor's terms and local laws. Review each privacy policy and consider self-hosting for confidential data.

Which is best for coding?

DeepSeek's R1 is the widely cited coding-reasoning pick, and Qwen's coder variants are strong and open. Both run locally via tools like Ollama. For most builders, start with one of these two.

Do they censor content?

Hosted Chinese models apply content filters per Chinese regulation, most visible on political topics. For technical, scientific, and business use the filtering is rarely in the way. Self-hosted open weights give you full control.

How do I access them outside China?

DeepSeek and Qwen are globally accessible (Qwen via Alibaba Cloud or open-weight hosts like Hugging Face). Kimi's web app works internationally once registered. Doubao and ERNIE are far more China-centric. Check current access in your region.
