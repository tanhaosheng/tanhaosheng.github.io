The number that should stop you

The FBI's IC3 2023 Internet Crime Report put reported losses from internet crime at $12.5 billion — and phishing and related confidence scams are among the largest drivers. That's reported losses only; the real total is higher because most attempts go unreported. I lead with the figure not to scare you but because it's the one fact that survives scrutiny — it comes from a government report, not a vendor's "90% of emails are threats" slide.

What a migration guide means here

You don't "buy" phishing protection like a product. You migrate your habits — and your organization's — from a click-first, reactive posture to a verify-first, defended one. Below, each step is a move from old behavior to new, with the reasoning behind it.

Step 1: Links → Verify the domain

Old: see a "package delivered" email, click the link, log in. New: hover (don't click) and read the actual domain — amaz0n-support.com is not amazon.com. Better: open the site by typing the address yourself. The display name lies; the URL usually doesn't.

Step 2: One password everywhere → Manager + MFA

Old: reuse a strong-ish password across sites, so one phish unlocks everything. New: a password manager that only autofills the real domain (it won't fill a lookalike), plus multi-factor authentication. Prefer phishing-resistant MFA — passkeys or hardware keys (FIDO) — over SMS codes, which SIM-swap attacks defeat.

Step 3: Trust the caller ID → Out-of-band verify

Old: "Microsoft support" calls, you believe the number. New: caller ID is trivially spoofed. Hang up and call the organization on a number you looked up independently. This single habit kills most "vishing" and "whaling" attempts targeting executives.

Step 4: One spam filter → Layered defense

Old: rely on the email provider's filter and assume the rest is safe. New: filter + reporting button + regular training. DMARC, DKIM, and SPF on your own domain (if you run email) cut spoofing of your brand. BIMI adds a verified logo. These are spec-level controls, not optional polish.

Step 5: Stay silent → Report

Old: delete and move on. New: forward to your provider's abuse address and report to the FBI IC3 and the Anti-Phishing Working Group. Aggregated reports are how the wider defense improves.

The AI wrinkle (and the skeptic's note)

AI has made phishing emails grammatically clean and enabled voice-cloning vishing and deepfake video. That's real and worth taking seriously. But be skeptical of vendors claiming "AI detects 99% of phishing" — detection rates that perfect are marketing, not measurement. The durable defenses above (verify domain, resist MFA, out-of-band confirmation) don't depend on any single detection tool and survive the AI twist.

Frequently asked questions

Is SMS two-factor good enough?

Better than nothing, but SIM-swapping and number-porting attacks let attackers intercept the code. Phishing-resistant options — passkeys or FIDO hardware keys — are the migration target. CISA and the FIDO Alliance both steer users toward them.

How do I spot a phishing email fast?

Three tells: urgency ("act in 10 minutes"), a mismatched real destination behind the link, and a request for credentials or payment. None alone is proof, but the combination is near-certain. When in doubt, verify through a channel you opened yourself.

My company got spoofed — what do I set up?

Publish SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on your domain, and consider BIMI for a verified logo. That stops attackers from sending mail that looks like it's from you, which protects your customers more than it protects you.

What should I do if I already clicked?

Disconnect from the network, change the password from a clean device (using a manager), enable MFA, and notify your bank or IT if anything financial was entered. Speed limits the damage — most account takeover is automated within minutes.

Verdict: Phishing defense in 2026 is less about a tool and more about a habit migration — from clicking to verifying, from reused passwords to managed ones with resistant MFA, from trusting caller ID to confirming out-of-band. The one number worth memorizing is the FBI's $12.5B, because it's real and it's growing. The caveat: no step here is a silver bullet, and the "AI detects everything" claims you'll hear are not supported by the public evidence — layer the basics and verify independently.
