The counterintuitive question

Why does a 2026 smart home still need three apps when "one standard to rule them all" was promised around 2014? The answer explains the entire history below: standards unify devices, not the interface layer. Vendors keep their apps for differentiation, and that gap is the through-line of 50 years of home automation.

Why the three-app problem persists

Matter (CSA) lets a bulb from vendor A respond to a hub from vendor B — at the device level. It does not mandate a single app. So a Matter-certified device still ships with its maker's app for firmware, scenes, and support, while also working through your hub. Result: more interop, same app sprawl. Thread (the IPv6 mesh Matter often runs on) solves the radio layer; it does not solve the UI layer.

A representative config note (illustrative)

For a local-first setup, Home Assistant's Matter integration registers a Thread border router and pairs devices. The pattern is a YAML entry such as:

matter:
  enable: true
  thread_stack: true # uses the border router for low-power devices

This is the standard integration shape; exact keys vary by release. The point for the analysis: local control is a configuration choice, not a default you get for free.

Selection, by priority

  • Privacy / local-first: Home Assistant Green or Apple Home. Accept setup effort.
  • Breadth / least friction: Amazon Alexa or Google Home. Accept cloud dependency.
  • Samsung household: SmartThings, which now runs largely hub-free.
  • Zigbee device base: Aqara hubs double as Matter border routers.

Frequently asked questions

Is Matter "done" in 2026?

Functionally mature for common device classes (lights, locks, sensors, plugs). Versions 1.3–1.4 added types and legacy bridging. A 2022 device won't gain 2026 features, but new buys are safe.

Which ecosystem is cheapest to start?

Effectively $0 if you use a phone as controller with cloud ecosystems, or ~$99 for a HomePod mini / Home Assistant Green anchor. The cost is in devices, not the hub.

Do I need a separate hub if I buy Matter devices?

Often no — many 2026 TVs, speakers, and routers act as Thread border routers. But for full local control without cloud, a dedicated local hub (Home Assistant, HomePod) is the reliable choice.

Will my old Zigbee/Z-Wave gear stop working?

No. Matter bridges legacy protocols; vendors ship bridge devices so Zigbee/Z-Wave products keep functioning inside a Matter setup.

Verdict: The 2026 smart home is more interoperable than any prior generation thanks to Matter and Thread, but the "one app" promise remains unmet because standards unify devices, not interfaces. For privacy and reliability, a local-first hub (Home Assistant or Apple Home) is the measured pick; for lowest friction, a cloud ecosystem wins. The caveat — every price above is official list and bundle-dependent; the linked sources are the authority, and "Matter-compatible" does not guarantee full local control without checking the hub.
