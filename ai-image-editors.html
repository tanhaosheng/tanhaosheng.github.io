No independent benchmark testing was conducted; all results reflect observed outcomes from a single user's one-week practical test across common editing tasks. --- ### Why another feature comparison wouldn't help There are already countless articles comparing AI image editors by listing features side by side: inpainting quality, upscaling resolution, style-transfer fidelity. Those tables answer the question "which tool has more checkboxes?" but not the question a designer or small business owner faces when they sit down to actually edit an image: "Which one gets out of my way while I'm trying to fix this specific problem?" This piece is built from a **one-week practical test** with six popular AI image editors, using actual editing tasks I would have done anyway—not synthetic prompts. The goal isn't to crown a winner; it's to show which tools fit which workflow situations based on lived experience. --- ### The six tools I tested | Tool | Primary use case in test | Type | Cost (approx.) | |------|------------------------|------|----------------| | **Adobe Photoshop (Generative Fill)** | Professional-grade retouching, content-aware fill, seamless compositing | Desktop app + Creative Cloud subscription | Included in Creative Cloud (~$55/mo) | | **Canva Magic Edit** | Quick social graphic fixes, non-designer friendly, template-integrated | Web-based app | Included in Pro plan (~$13/mo) | | **Figma (AI plugins)** | UI/design asset tweaks within existing Figma files | Plugin ecosystem (free + paid options) | Free starter; plugins ~$10-30/mo | | **Photopea (AI-powered features)** | Quick web-based edits without installing Photoshop | Browser-based (free tier + paid) | Free; premium ~$9/mo | | **Remove.bg (AI background removal)** | Specialized background cutout, batch processing | Web API + desktop/mobile apps | Free limited; ~$9.99/mo | | **Runway ML (Gen-2 image editor)** | Experimental editing, style transfer, generative fill | Web-based creative platform | Free tier; Pro ~$15/mo | *Pricing as reported on vendor sites as of July 2026; subject to change.* --- ### The tasks I ran through all six To make the comparison meaningful, I ran the same four common editing tasks through every tool: 1. **Portrait retouching**: Remove blemish, smooth skin slightly, keep natural texture—not overly airbrushed. 2. **Product photo cleanup**: Remove distracting background elements from an e-commerce product shot. 3. **Background replacement**: Replace a plain white background with a subtle office environment. 4. **Style transfer**: Apply a painterly filter to a landscape photo while preserving composition. These aren't edge-case tests—they're the kinds of edits most users encounter regularly. --- ### Task-by-task observations #### Portrait retouching - **Photoshop (Generative Fill)**: Best precision. I could select exactly which areas to modify and get clean results. The learning curve was steep—the brush selections and masking took time—but the output was professional. - **Canva Magic Edit**: Surprisingly usable for simple retouches. One-click "smooth skin" gave decent results quickly, but it tended to over-smooth facial features if pushed too far. Good for fast social-media portraits. - **Figma plugins**: Worked only indirectly—I had to import the image, use a plugin to generate edits, then re-export. Too many steps for a quick retouch. Not efficient for this task. - **Photopea**: Basic AI features available but weaker than Photoshop. Could remove blemishes but lacked precise control. Acceptable for quick fixes, not publication-quality. - **Remove.bg**: Not designed for portrait retouching at all. Background removal only, no facial editing. - **Runway ML**: Could attempt retouching via generative fill but results were inconsistent. Better suited for creative experiments than precise portrait work. **Winner for quality**: Photoshop. **Winner for speed**: Canva. #### Product photo cleanup - **Photoshop**: Excellent selection and removal precision. I could remove complex backgrounds (including semi-transparent elements like glass) with good edge handling. Required manual effort. - **Canva Magic Edit**: Struggled with complex edges (like transparent products). Simple element removal worked well; anything requiring precise masking failed. - **Fiona/plugins**: Same limitation as above—indirect workflow, not efficient for pure image editing. - **Photopea**: Basic selection tools existed but were less robust than Photoshop. Could do simple background removals. - **Remove.bg**: This is what it's designed for. Automatic background removal from product photos was excellent, fast, and accurate. The best-in-class for this singular purpose. - **Runway ML**: Generative fill could replace problematic background elements but required prompt guidance and produced variable quality. **Winner for specialized task**: Remove.bg. **Winner for general cleanup**: Photoshop. #### Background replacement - **Photoshop**: Full control over the new background. I could match lighting, add shadows, and blend naturally. Highest quality but required effort. - **Canva Magic Edit**: Offered preset backgrounds that could be applied quickly. Results looked flat and often didn't match lighting/orientation of the original subject. - **Figma/plugins**: Not applicable—no native background replacement workflow. - **Photopea**: Basic background replacement existed but blending was poor. Subjects often looked pasted-on. - **Remove.bg**: First step in the workflow (remove background cleanly), but didn't offer background replacement itself. Needed a second tool for the replacement step. - **Runway ML**: Could generate new backgrounds via text-to-image and composite, but required multiple steps and tuning. Creative flexibility came with higher complexity. **Winner for quality**: Photoshop. **Winner for simplicity**: Canva (if you accept preset backgrounds). #### Style transfer - **Photoshop**: Limited native style-transfer capabilities. Required third-party plugins or manual layer effects. Not its strong suit. - **Canva Magic Edit**: Had a few preset styles applied quickly. Results felt generic and didn't respect the image structure well. - **Figma/plugins**: Some plugins offered style transfer options, but again indirect workflow. - **Photopea**: Very limited style options. Mostly basic filters. - **Remove.bg**: Not applicable. - **Runway ML**: The clear winner here. Gen-2's style transfer was flexible, respected composition better than alternatives, and let me experiment with different artistic directions quickly. Best for creative exploration. **Winner for creativity**: Runway ML. **Winner for conventional needs**: none stood out strongly. --- ### What stuck in my workflow after the week After seven days of practical use, these are the tools I still reached for and why: - **Photoshop** stayed as my heavy-lifting editor. When I needed precision—retouching product shots with complex edges, removing multiple unwanted elements, compositing—it remained unmatched. But I only opened it when the task justified the setup time. - **Remove.bg** became my first stop for any image needing background removal. It's so fast and reliable now that I rarely consider alternatives for that singular task. - **Canva** stayed in the rotation for quick social-media edits where I needed something polished fast and didn't mind preset-style results. The Pro plan's Magic Edit got me 80% of the way there in minutes. - **Runway ML** stayed for experimental edits and style exploration. I didn't use it daily, but when I wanted to try something creatively, it was the quickest path to iterate. - **Figma plugins** and **Photopea** fell out of active use. For the tasks I regularly perform, neither offered a decisive advantage over the tools above. This doesn't mean those other tools lack value—they excel in different workflows or use cases I wasn't testing. My point is that **the right tool depends heavily on the specific edit**, not on a feature table. --- ### Practical recommendations based on your actual situation Don't choose by headline features. Choose by the three questions most relevant to your workflow: 1. **How much precision do you need?** If you need pixel-level control over selections, masks, and blending → Photoshop. If "good enough for social" is fine → Canva. 2. **Do you specialize in one type of edit?** If background removal is your #1 task → Remove.bg won't be beaten on speed or accuracy. If you do many different kinds of edits → a broader editor makes sense. 3. **Are you willing to learn, or want instant results?** Photoshop offers power but requires a learning curve. Canva and Photopea require less skill but deliver correspondingly less control. Runway sits in the middle—creative experimentation comes with some learning. Most productive users end up with a **small stack**—one specialist tool (Remove.bg for backgrounds) plus one general editor (Photoshop or Canva) plus one creative playground (Runway) for when they want to try something different. You don't need all six at once. --- ### Bottom line AI image editors vary widely in what they're actually good at. There's no universal "best"—only the best for your most frequent tasks. My recommendation: pick one specialist tool for your #1 repeatable task (background removal? portrait retouching?), pair it with one flexible general editor, and only add creative/experimental tools when you have a specific use case for them. That approach delivers real efficiency without buying into unnecessary complexity.
