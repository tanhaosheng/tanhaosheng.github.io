Spaced Repetition: Master Long-Term Memory

Spaced repetition is a learning technique that involves reviewing information at gradually increasing intervals. It exploits the psychological spacing effect to maximize memory retention.

The Science Behind Spaced Repetition

Our brains forget information over time following the forgetting curve described by Hermann Ebbinghaus. Spaced repetition interrupts this curve by reviewing material just before it would be forgotten, strengthening the memory each time.

Optimal Review Intervals

Typical intervals start short and grow longer. Review new material after one day, then three days, then one week, then two weeks, then one month. The exact schedule depends on the difficulty of the material and your learning goals.

Digital Tools for Spaced Repetition

Anki is the most popular spaced repetition application, using an algorithm that adapts to your performance. Other options include Quizlet, Memrise, and SuperMemo. These tools automate scheduling so you can focus on learning.

Creating Effective Flashcards

Use simple, atomic cards that test one piece of information each. Include both the question and answer on the same card. Add images, mnemonics, and examples to make cards memorable. Avoid complex multi-part questions.

Integrating into Your Study Routine

Set aside 15 to 30 minutes daily for spaced repetition. Consistency is more important than duration. Review cards during downtime like commuting or waiting in line. Track your retention rate and adjust card difficulty as needed.
