"Free" is not a price — it's a meter

Every coding assistant markets a free tier, and almost none of them mean the same thing. Cursor's free plan is a genuine hobby tier you can keep forever. Codeium's individual plan is free with no time limit. GitHub Copilot's "free" is a 14-day trial that then asks for $10 a month — unless you're a student or an open-source maintainer, in which case it stays free. Calling all three "free" in one breath hides the actual difference, which is whether the meter ever runs out.

The question worth asking isn't "which is free?" but "what does the free part actually cover, and where does it stop?" That's the matrix below.

What each one really is

Cursor — the local-first fork. Cursor is a VS Code–based editor that lets you point at either its cloud models or a local/open model like DeepSeek-Coder or a Llama variant. The honest draw of the free tier isn't the cloud model (quotas are tight); it's that you can run inference on your own hardware and keep source files on your machine. Community reports put a 33B-class local model at roughly 8–12 tokens/second on a consumer GPU — usable for day-to-day editing, not for blasting out a whole module. The catch is the GPU: no decent card, no local speed. Pricing: free Hobby tier, Pro at $20/month for heavier cloud use (cursor.com/pricing).

Codeium — the no-strings free tier. Codeium's individual plan is free and stays free, covering autocomplete, chat, and a broad set of languages across most editors. The trade community members report is consistency: the same prompt can return different-quality completions across sessions, and deep multi-file "project understanding" sits behind the paid Pro tier (~$15/month, codeium.com/pricing). For a student or a casual scripter, the free tier is genuinely enough. For someone maintaining a large codebase, the ceiling shows up fast.

GitHub Copilot — the timed taste. Copilot's individual plan is $10/month (github.com/features/copilot/plans), but it's free for verified students and open-source maintainers, and it offers a 14-day trial to everyone else. During that window you get the strongest model of the three and the tightest GitHub integration — PR summaries, issue context, in-editor chat. After day 14 it's a bill or a downgrade. The privacy note matters: during use, your code and prompts go to Microsoft's servers, so it's a poor fit for proprietary or regulated code without checking your employer's policy.

Pick by your situation

  • You handle proprietary code and can't send it to the cloud: Cursor on local models. It's the only one of the three whose free tier keeps everything on your disk.
  • You're learning, or you just need completions and chat at zero cost forever: Codeium. No trial clock, no card.
  • You want to feel the best model before paying: Copilot's 14-day trial, then decide if $10/month earns its keep against your GitHub workflow.
  • You're a student or you maintain popular OSS: Copilot, free permanently — apply through GitHub's education and open-source programs.

Two myths worth killing

"Local models are as good as GPT-4." They aren't, for hard reasoning. They're good enough for completion, refactoring boilerplate, and explaining your own code back to you. Treat them as a fast junior pair-programmer who's read your files, not as an architect.

"Free tier means no lock-in." The opposite: the free tier is the hook. Cursor's value lives in its editor; Codeium's in its plugins; Copilot's in GitHub. You're not buying features, you're renting a workflow. That's fine — just know which one you're standing on.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cursor actually free?

The Hobby tier is free with no expiry and covers local-model completion, chat, and the Auto-Fix feature. Cloud-model usage on the free tier is rate-limited; unlimited cloud access needs the $20/month Pro plan. Details shift, so check cursor.com/pricing.

What happens when Copilot's trial ends?

You're moved to the paid individual plan at $10/month (or $19/user/month for Business). Students and verified open-source maintainers keep free access through GitHub's programs. You can also simply stop — your code isn't held hostage.

Will my code leave my machine?

On Cursor's local mode, no. On Copilot and Codeium's default cloud modes, snippets and prompts are sent to their servers for completion. If that's a problem for your work, run Cursor locally or self-host an open model through it.

Which has the best non-English support?

Cursor, wired to DeepSeek and other China-optimized models, handles Chinese instructions well locally. Copilot's trial model is also strong on Chinese. Codeium trails the other two on non-English prompting per community reports.

Verdict: There is no "best free coding assistant" — there's the one whose free part matches your constraint. If the constraint is privacy, Cursor on local models wins by default. If it's cost with no expiry, Codeium. If it's model quality for a two-week evaluation, Copilot's trial. The caveat: every free tier is a timed or limited on-ramp to a paid workflow, so choose the one you'd actually be willing to pay for if the meter starts running.
