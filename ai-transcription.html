Most "AI transcription" is the same engine in a different coat

Here's the claim I'll defend: the majority of consumer transcription apps are wrappers around OpenAI's Whisper or a Whisper-class model, and the things that actually separate them are not "accuracy" — it's price, how fast you get the text, whether speaker labels are real or guessed, and what the vendor does with your audio afterward. If you optimize for the wrong axis, you'll overpay for a model you could run yourself.

The opinionated takes

Whisper is the floor, not a product. OpenAI's Whisper is open-weight and free to run locally (github.com/openai/whisper). If your audio is clean and you have any technical comfort, you can transcribe indefinitely at zero cost and zero privacy leakage. The downside is that you're the product team: no speaker labels, no meeting bot, no search. For a lone researcher, that's a fine trade. For a team, it's a project.

Otter.ai owns live meetings. Otter's free tier gives real-time captions and searchable notes; the Pro plan (around $8.33/month billed annually, otter.ai/pricing) adds more monthly transcription minutes and import capabilities. Where it earns its keep is the live captioning loop — turn up to a meeting and leave with a searchable record. Community reports note accuracy drops on heavy accents and overlapping speakers, which is true of Whisper-class models generally.

Fireflies is for people who hate updating the CRM. Fireflies' bot joins your calls, transcribes, and can push summaries into Salesforce or HubSpot (fireflies.ai/pricing). Free tier exists; paid Pro is around $10/month. The opinion: if your pain is "nobody logs the call," this is the right tool. If your pain is "I need perfect quotes for an article," it isn't — the summaries are convenient but you should still read the raw transcript.

Rev is what you reach for when it has to be right. Rev layers human review on AI output. Their AI transcription runs roughly $0.015/min; human-verified is about $1.25/min (rev.com/pricing). That's 80x the price for a much lower error floor. Use it for legal, medical, or published quotes — not for your weekly standup.

AssemblyAI and Trint are the pro tier. AssemblyAI is a developer API with speaker diarization and redaction built in, billed by the minute (assemblyai.com/pricing) — you build the UX. Trint gives journalists a correction-first editor and collaborative workflow from about $60/month (trint.com/pricing). Different buyers, same truth: you're paying for the editing and the accountability, not the raw model.

What the marketing won't tell you

  • Diarization is the real feature gap. "Who said what" is harder than "what was said," and free tiers often guess or skip it. If speaker labels matter, check that explicitly.
  • Your audio is the vendor's training fuel unless stated otherwise. Read the privacy terms. Local Whisper avoids this entirely.
  • Accuracy on clean audio is a solved problem; on crosstalk it isn't. Don't trust a vendor's headline WER on a podcast recorded in a quiet studio and assume your noisy three-person call matches it.

Frequently asked questions

Is Whisper really free?

The open-weight model is free to run on your own hardware. Cloud services that wrap Whisper (or similar models) charge for convenience, storage, and features. If you have a machine and basic command-line comfort, $0 is real.

Which tool is best for Zoom/Teams meetings?

Otter and Fireflies both join calls and produce searchable transcripts; Fireflies adds CRM sync, Otter adds stronger live captions. For pure personal recall, Otter's free tier is enough; for pipeline automation, Fireflies.

How private is my audio with cloud tools?

It depends on the vendor's retention and training policy — read the privacy page. If confidentiality is non-negotiable, run Whisper locally so the audio never leaves your machine.

Do these handle multiple languages?

Whisper-class models handle dozens of languages. Accuracy varies by language and by how much that language appeared in training data. Test your specific language on a real sample before committing to a paid plan.

Verdict: If you're technical and privacy-minded, run Whisper locally and stop paying. If you need live meeting notes, Otter. If you need CRM automation, Fireflies. If the transcript must be defensible, Rev's human tier. The caveat: every wrapper shares the same underlying engine, so pay for the workflow and the accountability — not for a word-error-rate number you can't verify.
