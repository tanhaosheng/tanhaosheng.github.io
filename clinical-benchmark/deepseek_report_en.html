3. Three Systematic Failures (every run, the main point loss)

① Never questions the staging (1.1+1.2, all 16 points lost)
Pathology states "3 clusters of stromal invasion, largest focus 0.2 cm = 2 mm". Microinvasion is defined as a single focus ≤1 mm; 0.2 cm already exceeds microinvasion → should be at least pT1a (Stage IA). All three runs copied the discharge "TisN0M0 Stage 0" without flagging the ambiguity. This is the biggest clinical safety-level error: a staging mismatch cascades into treatment intensity, follow-up cadence, and patient expectations. (Run 3 used the term "DCIS with microinvasion" but still did not correct Stage 0.)
② Post-mastectomy DCIS endocrine recommendation not downgraded (3.2a, all 12 points lost)
Guideline P59 note a states: for DCIS after mastectomy, endocrine drugs belong to the "chemoprevention" category, with the recommendation level downgraded to "consider / optional". All three runs applied the "DCIS → endocrine is standard / strongly recommended" template, without distinguishing mastectomy vs breast-conserving surgery. This is the highest-discrimination core item in the framework; total failure.
③ 60-year age cutoff for AI vs TAM not mentioned (3.2b, all 10 points lost)
Guideline P59 note b: AI's advantage over TAM is mainly seen in postmenopausal women <60; at ≥60 the two are nearly equivalent, clinically either is acceptable (slight lean to AI). The patient is exactly 60, at the boundary. Run1/Run3 directly gave "AI first choice", Run2 said "either after menopause" — none wrote out the age cutoff, showing no fine reading of the guideline.

4. Other Key Findings

✅ What it got right (strengths)

  • Correct chemo-exemption judgment: all three correctly gave "no chemo needed"; Run1 also proactively mentioned Oncotype DX multigene testing for risk stratification (beyond framework expectation).
  • Follow-up plan mostly adequate: Run3 explicitly stated "routine tumor markers (CEA/CA15-3) not recommended", consistent with the guideline, better than the other two.
  • Reasonable safety awareness: all mentioned AI-related bone-density/lipid monitoring, calcium and Vit D.
  • Extremely consistent: zero variance across 3 runs, meaning output is templated not random, and thus predictable.

⚠️ Structural weaknesses

  • Guideline-section citation accuracy = 0 / 16: all three only say "mainstream guideline / latest 2026 / authoritative guideline", with no single reference to a specific 2026 CBCS section or page. You cannot verify it actually read the guideline vs applied generic knowledge — a hard hit to clinical credibility.
  • Structured output failed: the Prompt asked for "bullets + section/page citations + 5-block structure"; none delivered verifiable section citations.
  • Templated phrasing: all open with the safe boilerplate "very early stage / low risk / Luminal type", like pattern matching rather than clinical reasoning.
  • No self-assessment (item 5 all lost): no run stated "confidence level" or "what is clinical reasoning vs guideline".

5. Practical Conclusion for Clinical Use

  • DeepSeek Fast mode is good for: quick Q&A, drug常识, follow-up reminders, patient-education phrasing — its conclusions "don't crash on the big picture" and it replies instantly.
  • Do not rely on it for: stage re-interpretation, recommendation-grade fine-tuning, age/boundary-related nuanced decisions — exactly where it systematically lost points, and it appears very confident, which is misleading.
  • Key reminder: when a case has "pathology description inconsistent with discharge diagnosis" (like this invasion focus vs Tis), no free-tier LLM should be trusted alone; a clinician must review.
