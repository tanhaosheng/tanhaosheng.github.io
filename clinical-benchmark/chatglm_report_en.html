4. ChatGLM's Two Independent Critical Flaws

① All-English output (usability flaw)
Input was a Chinese Prompt + Chinese case, yet all three answers were English. For Chinese clinicians this means: - High reading/review cost (clinical terms must be checked word by word); - Chinese guideline citations, term translation, and dosage notation may be lost/mistranslated; - It hands the decision to a model that "did not think in your language".
Clinically unacceptable, regardless of score.
② Extremely low information density + high instability (quality flaw)
Three runs averaged ~130 words (DeepSeek ~450), with almost no concrete clinical decisions; Run 2 scored only 2 (basically restating the case + "let the doctor decide"). Spread = 8, triggering the framework's "≥8 = unstable" threshold, meaning repeating the same question yields unpredictable results.

5. Final Recommendation (Framework §6)

Overall ranking

DeepSeek Fast mode ≫ ChatGLM 5.2 — DeepSeek is stronger on every comparable dimension, and is Chinese-output that can be directly clinically reviewed.

✅ One-line conclusion: In the triangle of "Chinese + clinical decision reasoning + zero cost", DeepSeek Fast mode decisively beats ChatGLM 5.2. Neither model reaches the threshold of being trusted alone clinically, but DeepSeek at least gives a "wrong-but-deep" reasoning chain to verify; ChatGLM gives "vague + disclaimer" with no verification value and poor fit for Chinese doctors' daily use.
