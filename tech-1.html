More cores isn't the answer

More cores is better — except when it isn't. The CPU that tops a multi-core chart often loses the task you actually do, because most desktop software still leans on a few fast threads. That gap is the whole Intel-vs-AMD argument, and it's why a spec sheet alone won't decide it for you.

Public benchmark trends (qualitative)

In the public benchmark families — Geekbench 6 for cross-platform single/multi, Cinebench R23 for rendering threads, PassMark for an aggregate CPU score — the pattern is consistent rather than decisive: AMD tends to lead on multi-core value in the mainstream and high-core bands; Intel remains competitive to leading on single-thread in several sub-segments and owns the deepest laptop/Thunderbolt ecosystem. Neither "wins" universally. The honest read is by workload, not by brand.

Real scenarios

  • Gaming at 1080p: single-thread and cache matter most; either vendor's current flagship is fine, and a fast GPU usually dominates the result.
  • Video / 3D rendering: more cores win; AMD's high-core Ryzen and 3D-V-Cache parts are strong value here.
  • Office + light use: either mainstream part; iGPU quality and platform features tip it.
  • Upgrade-minded builder: AM5's longer socket support reduces future motherboard cost.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for gaming?

At the top, it's close; the GPU matters more than the CPU brand. For cache-sensitive titles, AMD's 3D-V-Cache parts are frequently cited as strong. Match the CPU to your monitor's refresh and your GPU, not to a logo.

Is Intel "faster"?

In some single-thread sub-segments, yes; in many multi-core value bands, no. Public benchmarks show the lead trading by workload. Buy the workload, not the claim.

Which is better value?

AMD has often led on cores-per-dollar in the mainstream and high-core tiers; Intel competes hard in mid segments and leads in laptop ecosystem breadth. Price the exact SKU you'd buy, not the tier.

Does the iGPU matter?

If you won't add a graphics card, yes — both ship capable integrated graphics sufficient for display, video, and light work. For gaming or pro graphics, a discrete GPU is still the call.

Verdict: There is no "better CPU," only the better CPU for the workload and the upgrade plan. Intel leads where single-thread and laptop ecosystem matter; AMD leads where multi-core value and a long-lived socket matter. The caveat: benchmark leads trade hands by segment and by quarter — price the exact SKU against your real task, and ignore any absolute "Intel beats AMD" or vice versa claim.
