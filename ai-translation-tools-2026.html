The number that reframes the whole category

DeepL's individual Pro plan listed at roughly €8.74 per month in 2025 — less than a single hour of professional human translation, which typically runs tens of dollars once you account for per-word rates. That ratio is why "which translation tool" stopped being a question only enterprises asked. The tools are now cheap enough that the real decision is about fit, not cost.

What each one is actually for

  • DeepL wins on reading quality for European languages. Operators consistently report its output reads more naturally than machine translation used to, especially German, French, and Japanese. Document translation that preserves layout is a genuine differentiator for anyone handling contracts or decks. The catch: thinner language coverage and a smaller free allowance than Google.
  • Google Translate wins on reach — more languages than anyone, free for casual use, and a mature Cloud API. If you need to cover a language DeepL doesn't, Google is the default. Quality on major languages is strong; on rare pairs it can drift.
  • Microsoft Translator wins inside Microsoft shops. If your documents live in Office 365 and your stack is Azure, the integration is the selling point, and the per-character API price is competitive.
  • Amazon Translate wins when translation is one step in an AWS pipeline. You wouldn't adopt it standalone, but if you're already in Lambda/S3, the ~$15/1M-char rate and tight integration remove a separate vendor.

The part nobody mentions: post-editing

Public reports from localization teams agree on one thing — raw machine output still needs a human pass for anything customer-facing. The tools differ in how much they reduce that pass, not in eliminating it. DeepL tends to shorten the edit for European languages; Google tends to widen your language reach. Pick by which problem you actually have.

Frequently asked questions

Is DeepL really better than Google Translate?

For major European languages, many operators find DeepL reads more naturally. For language coverage and free casual use, Google is broader. "Better" depends on the pair and the reader.

What does an API actually cost at volume?

List rates cluster around $10–20 per million characters. At 10 million chars/month that's roughly $100–200 — still far below human translation. Check each vendor's pricing page for tier discounts.

Can these handle PDFs and keep formatting?

DeepL translates documents while preserving layout on paid plans; Google and Microsoft handle docs through their office/cloud integrations. Complex layouts still benefit from a human check.

Are translations private?

Each vendor publishes data-handling terms that differ by plan — free tiers may use data for improvement, paid/enterprise tiers typically offer stronger isolation. Read the specific policy for your plan before sending confidential text.
