The number that doesn't exist

Open almost any "best AI resume builder" page and you'll see a table: Jobscan — ATS score 94%. Teal — 89%. Here is the uncomfortable part: no employer's applicant-tracking system outputs a 0–100 "ATS score" for your resume. Workday, Greenhouse, Taleo, and Lever parse your resume into fields and match keywords against the job description. They don't grade you. The "score" these tools show is their own metric — usually how well your resume's keywords line up with a JD you pasted in. Useful as a checklist, misleading as a verdict.

What an ATS actually does

An applicant-tracking system is a parser and a filter, not a judge. In practice it:

  • Extracts your resume into structured fields (name, employers, skills) — and chokes on tables, columns, and images.
  • Matches those fields against the job's required keywords.
  • Surfaces candidates to a recruiter, who then reads the actual document.

So the two things that genuinely help are: (1) a clean, parseable format, and (2) the right keywords present and truthful. An AI builder helps with both — but it cannot manufacture the interview, and it cannot invent experience you don't have.

What each one is genuinely good at

Teal is the sensible starting point because the free tier is usable: a clean builder, a job tracker, and keyword-matching against a saved description. Pay only if you want the resume-score and bulk features.

Jobscan does one job narrowly and well — paste a posting, see which required keywords your resume is missing. That's the "match rate" you see; treat it as a gap checklist, not a grade. It's most valuable when you're tailoring one resume to many similar roles.

Kickresume wins on design and ease. If your field cares about visual polish (creative, marketing, some founders), its templates export cleanly to PDF. Less of a "keyword engine," more of a "make it look right" tool.

Rezi leans on AI rewriting to restructure bullet points into ATS-friendly phrasing. Handy if you struggle to phrase achievements — but the content still has to be yours.

Three job seekers, three setups

  • New grad, no paid experience: Use Teal's free builder. Lead with projects, coursework, and skills; run Jobscan's match against each posting. Don't pay yet.
  • Mid-career switcher: Jobscan to re-align keywords toward the target field; Rezi or Kickresume to phrase transferable wins. Budget ~$30–50 for one focused month.
  • Senior exec: Design and brevity matter more than keyword tricks. Kickresume for layout, a human editor for judgment. An AI tool polishes; it doesn't decide what's worth saying.

Frequently asked questions

Will an AI resume builder get me past the ATS?

It helps in two real ways: cleaner formatting (so the parser extracts everything) and better keyword coverage (so you match the JD). It does not guarantee an interview — a human still reads the result.

What's a "good" ATS score, then?

There isn't a standardized one. Any score you see is the tool's own keyword-match percentage against a job description you provided. Use it to find missing keywords; don't treat 90% as a pass and 70% as a fail.

Free vs paid — when to upgrade?

Start free. Pay for one month only when you're in active, high-volume applications and a specific feature (Jobscan's matching, Rezi's rewriting) is clearly saving you time.

Can it write the content for me?

It can draft and rephrase, but you must supply real achievements. A tool that invents responsibilities for a job you never held is a lie on your resume, and lies surface in the interview.

Fix your content first (real, quantified achievements), then use Teal or Jobscan free to align it to each posting, and only pay for a focused month if you're applying heavily. Ignore any "ATS score" that isn't tied to a specific job description you pasted in.
