Every generation promised a revolution. Most underdelivered.

1G put phones in cars. 2G made them pocket-sized and added texts. 3G brought data; 4G made video stream; 5G was going to dissolve the line between the physical and digital world — autonomous factories, remote surgery, 20 Gbps in your hand. We're at 2026, and here's what 5G actually delivered to most people: a marginal speed bump and a battery hit. The revolution is still, conspicuously, elsewhere.

That gap — between the keynote and the sidewalk — is the most useful thing to understand before anyone sells you 6G. So let's autopsy 5G first, then look at what 6G really is.

The 20 Gbps "peak" is a real ITU IMT-2020 requirement — for lab conditions, not your commute. See ITU M.2083.

None of this makes 5G a failure. It makes it a normal generation: solid infrastructure, oversold narrative. The lesson for 6G is to read the spec sheet, not the slogan.

What 6G actually is today (2026)

6G is a research program with a published shape, not a product. The agreed anchors:

  • Timeline: ITU's IMT-2030 framework targets standardization through the late 2020s and first commercial deployments around 2030. 3GPP's relevant releases (Release 21 and beyond) are slated in that window — not 2026.
  • Spectrum: 6G research centers on terahertz bands (roughly 100 GHz to 3 THz), which open enormous bandwidth but travel badly — measured in meters, not kilometers, without dense small-cell builds.
  • Speed target: Public research goals cite peak rates on the order of 1 Tbps in lab conditions. That is a target, not a measured consumer experience.
  • AI-native: Unlike prior generations where intelligence was bolted on, 6G's design assumes AI in scheduling, spectrum use, and slicing from the start.

The honest read

6G will matter — for industrial sensing, holographic telepresence, and networks that allocate themselves. It will not reach your phone as a life-changing upgrade in 2026, 2027, or likely 2028. Anyone telling you otherwise is reading a press release, not a spec.

Frequently asked questions

When will 6G be available to consumers?

Not before roughly 2030 per the ITU/3GPP roadmap. Early trials and pre-standard hardware will appear before then, but commercial handsets are a late-2020s story at the earliest. See 3GPP.

Is 6G 100 times faster than 5G?

"100x" is a marketing shorthand, not a measured consumer figure. Lab peak targets reach into the terabit range, but real-world speeds depend on spectrum, cell density, and device power — none of which exist yet for 6G.

Should I buy a 6G-ready phone now?

There are no 6G phones in 2026. Buy for the 5G/4G performance and features you need today; "6G ready" claims this early are premature.

What's the real difference from 5G?

Theoretical spectrum (terahertz vs. cm-wave), tighter AI integration into the network fabric, and design goals aimed at sensing and joint communication-sensing rather than just faster downloads.
