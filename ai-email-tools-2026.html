No independent benchmarking was conducted; all performance claims reflect observed results from a single user's week-long test. --- ### Why I spent a week with email assistants Email outreach is one of those tasks where "AI efficiency" gets tossed around a lot. Tools promise "perfectly personalized messages in seconds," but the reality usually falls somewhere between "I still have to write half of it" and "the outputs sound suspiciously generic." I wanted to see where the truth actually lies for a practical sales/marketing context, so I set aside one week and used four different AI email assistants for my actual outreach work—no synthetic prompts, no test scenarios, just what I would have sent anyway. Here is the week in practice, what I tried, and what I kept. --- ### The workflow I tested on My typical outreach week breaks into four activities: 1. **Drafting cold emails** from a prospect list (roughly 40 messages per week) 2. **Personalizing** those messages with prospect-specific details (company name, recent product launch, relevant pain point) 3. **A/B testing** subject lines and opening lines on a subset of recipients 4. **Analyzing** open/click-through patterns to adjust subsequent rounds I ran each tool through this same workflow and compared the outputs side by side. The tools I evaluated were: **Lavender**, **Superhuman (AI features)**, **HubSpot AI**, and **Copy.ai for outreach**. I used each for roughly one day of actual work. --- ### Day 1 — Lavender: "Optimization at the keystroke" Lavender positions itself less as a "generative AI email writer" and more as an AI-powered email optimizer that works inside your existing email client. During my week, I used its Gmail extension to draft and send cold outreach. - **Drafting**: I wrote the core message myself; Lavender's "AI optimize" button then suggested tweaks to subject lines, sentence structure, and length. It flagged certain phrases as "could be clearer" and offered alternatives. - **Personalization**: It offered one-click insertion of prospect name and company, but deeper personalization (mentioning a recent product launch) still required me to find and insert the detail manually. - **A/B testing**: Lavender's built-in A/B test feature let me send two subject lines to small segments and automatically route the winner to the remainder. The interface was straightforward, though the sample size recommendations felt conservative. - **Analysis**: Post-send, Lavender provided open/click rates and flagged messages that underperformed with suggestions for improvement. **What worked**: The subject-line optimization nudges were genuinely helpful—several that I would have sent unchanged got adjusted to higher open rates. The A/B test workflow integrated cleanly into my existing process. **What didn't**: The deeper personalization suggestions felt shallow; it couldn't meaningfully incorporate specific company research without me feeding it that info first. For a team that already researches prospects thoroughly, this wasn't a bottleneck. --- ### Day 2 — Superhuman (AI features): "Speed within the client" Superhuman is primarily an email client with AI features baked in. Its "AI rewrite" and "AI expand" commands work inside the editor as you type. - **Drafting**: I started with a rough outline in Superhuman, then used "AI expand" to flesh it into a full draft. The output was clean, conversational, and mostly ready to send with minor tweaks. - **Personalization**: Like Lavender, Superhuman didn't autonomously pull prospect-specific context—it needed me to paste that info in before generating personalized content. - **A/B testing**: Superhuman doesn't have native A/B testing for email; that had to be handled separately through my email platform. - **Analysis**: No built-in analytics; I relied on my email service provider's reports. **What worked**: The rewrite/expand commands were fast and produced readable text that required less editing than generic LLM outputs. The tight integration with the email client meant less switching. **What didn't**: The lack of native A/B testing and analysis meant I had to juggle multiple tools anyway. The AI felt more like a text generator than an email workflow assistant. --- ### Day 3 — HubSpot AI: "Inside the CRM" HubSpot AI lives within the CRM platform, so it assumes you're already using HubSpot for contact management and outreach tracking. - **Drafting**: HubSpot's email assistant suggested entire email bodies based on a short prompt and the contact's profile data (if available in the CRM). The tone varied from formal to conversational depending on the selected setting. - **Personalization**: This was HubSpot's strongest area—if the CRM had fields for company, role, or past interactions, those could be merged into the draft automatically. - **A/B testing**: HubSpot has built-in A/B testing for email subject lines and content, and the AI could suggest variations to test. - **Analysis**: Strong analytics integration: open rates, click rates, and reply rates all connected back to CRM records. **What worked**: The CRM integration made personalization and analysis seamless. If you're already in the HubSpot ecosystem, the AI features fit naturally and cut down on context switching. **What didn't**: If you're not using HubSpot CRM, the value proposition diminishes considerably. Setting up the CRM and connecting data sources required time investment that a solo practitioner might not have. --- ### Day 4 — Copy.ai for outreach: "Pure generative output" Copy.ai is a general-purpose generative AI tool with an outreach template. I used it independently of any email client. - **Drafting**: I gave a short prompt (audience, goal, key points), and Copy.ai generated 3-5 full email variations. The quality ranged from decent to awkward; the best ones needed only light editing. - **Personalization**: Copy.ai allowed inserting merge fields ({{name}}, {{company}}), but again, I had to supply the context itself. - **A/B testing**: No native A/B testing; generated variations had to be manually tested in my email platform. - **Analysis**: None; completely standalone. **What worked**: For raw draft generation, Copy.ai produced usable options quickly. The variety of tones was helpful when I wanted different angles on the same message. **What didn't**: As a standalone tool, it disconnected from the actual sending and tracking workflow. I still needed my email client and analytics platform, so Copy.ai was one piece of a larger puzzle. --- ### What I actually kept After the week, I evaluated which tool features I found myself returning to: | Feature | Tool | Used weekly? | Verdict | |---------|------|-------------|---------| | Subject-line optimization | Lavender | ✅ Yes | genuinely improved open rates | | One-click personalization merge | HubSpot AI | ✅ Yes (if CRM data exists) | valuable when CRM is already in use | | Draft expansion/rewrite | Superhuman | ✅ Occasionally | fast for quick messages | | Multiple draft variations | Copy.ai | ✅ Occasionally | useful for creative angles | | A/B test workflow | Lavender / HubSpot | ✅ Yes | built-in is convenient | | Post-send analysis | Lavender / HubSpot | ✅ Yes | insights inform next round | My current stack: I use **Lavender for subject-line optimization and A/B testing** (because it works directly inside Gmail), **HubSpot when I need CRM-integrated personalization** (and I already have HubSpot for other workflows), and **Superhuman's AI rewrite for quick responses** when I'm using Superhuman as my client. Copy.ai occasionally generates a fresh angle when I'm stuck. --- ### Lessons from the week - **AI that sits inside your email client** (Lavender, Superhuman) produced smoother workflows than tools that required context switching (Copy.ai). The fewer taps between "draft" and "send," the more likely you'll actually use the AI. - **Personalization is only as good as the data you feed it**. All tools required me to supply prospect-specific information; none autonomously pulled that from public sources in my test. The tools that integrated with existing CRM data (HubSpot) had a clear advantage there. - **Subject-line optimization showed measurable impact**. In my week, Lavender's nudges improved open rates by an estimated 10-15% on tested messages—a tangible win that felt worth the subscription. - **A/B testing is more useful if it's built-in**. Having to export a variation to a separate testing platform added friction. Built-in A/B features (Lavender, HubSpot) made experimentation easier. - **No tool replaced the need for human review**. Every draft I sent required at least some human edit—either to inject specific context, adjust tone, or verify accuracy. AI got me closer to "send-ready" faster, but not all the way there. --- ### Bottom line AI email tools don't magically write perfect cold emails. They do something arguably more useful: they remove small friction points—subject-line polishing, quick rewrites, A/B test automation—that add up over dozens of messages per week. If you're spending significant time on email outreach, the tools that integrate directly into your email client and offer measurable nudges (subject-line optimization, built-in A/B testing) delivered the clearest value in my test. The rest were helpful as occasional aids but not essential to the workflow.
