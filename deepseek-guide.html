The uncomfortable question

Here's an uncomfortable question: what if the cheapest, most open AI model is also the one you should trust the least with certain jobs? DeepSeek is genuinely remarkable — a reasoning model that rivals Western frontier labs at a fraction of the cost, with open weights. But "cheap and capable" is not the same as "safe to depend on for everything." This guide skips the hype and audits what can actually go wrong.

Three myths worth dropping

Myth 1: "It's free, so it's the best for everything." The free consumer app is real and generous, but free does not mean superior. DeepSeek trails Western labs on multimodal understanding — it reads text via OCR but doesn't truly see images — and its training skew toward Chinese and English means other languages get rougher output.

Myth 2: "It's Chinese, so it's spying on you." Nuanced, not binary. DeepSeek states that queries from outside China are routed to Singapore-based infrastructure. The real consideration is jurisdictional: a Chinese lab is subject to Chinese law, which shapes data-handling and content policy. That's a fact to weigh, not a cartoon villain.

Myth 3: "It beats GPT-4 at everything." On specific math and code benchmarks, R1 is competitive. On broad general assistance, vision, and tool integration, the Western incumbents still lead. "Competitive on some axes" is the honest claim.

Using it via API (real, OpenAI-compatible)

DeepSeek's API mirrors OpenAI's schema, so existing code ports with a base-URL swap. This is a genuine, documented call shape:

curl https://api.deepseek.com/chat/completions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $DEEPSEEK_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "deepseek-chat",
    "messages": [
      {"role": "user", "content": "Explain Multi-head Latent Attention in two sentences."}
    ]
  }'

Replace the env var with your key from the platform. The "model" field accepts deepseek-chat or deepseek-reasoner.

When it's the right call

  • Best for: budget-sensitive coding, long Chinese/English text analysis, and anyone who wants to self-host open weights.
  • Wrong for: image-heavy tasks, legally sensitive queries, or anything where a wrong confident answer is costly and unverified.

Frequently asked questions

Is DeepSeek safe to use day to day?

For ordinary writing and coding, yes. The standing advice applies to every cloud AI: don't paste passwords, ID numbers, or confidential material. Weigh the data-jurisdiction point if your queries are sensitive.

Is the free app really unlimited?

The consumer web and mobile app have been free with no per-message cap since launch. The company monetizes via API. Verify current terms on the official app, since free tiers can change.

Can I run it on my own hardware?

Yes — V3 and R1 weights are published on HuggingFace, so self-hosting is possible if you have the VRAM. That removes the data-jurisdiction risk entirely, at the cost of your own compute.

How does it compare to ChatGPT for coding?

R1 is competitive on algorithmic and debugging benchmarks (the SWE-Bench-class family). ChatGPT's edge is ecosystem: plugins, image understanding, and broader tooling. Pick by whether you need raw reasoning cheap or an integrated toolbox.

Use it freely for coding and text analysis where you can verify the output; keep a second model and never paste secrets where jurisdiction is a concern. The caveat: its confident hallucinations and topic-based refusals are real, so it earns reliance only where you're checking its work.
