"Note app" is three different tools

Call them all note apps and you've already made the error. Notion is a database engine with a text box bolted on. Obsidian is a folder of plain-text Markdown files with a graph view. Evernote is a search engine for things you clip and forget. They overlap at "I typed something and saved it," then diverge completely. The question isn't "which is best" — it's "which one is built for the person doing the work?"

What the user reports actually say

Strip the vendor copy and the recurring complaints are consistent:

  • Notion: loved for flexibility, resented when workspaces grow past ~10,000 blocks and the interface lags. Cloud-only means offline is unreliable — a real problem on a train.
  • Obsidian: praised for ownership and speed; the friction is the setup tax. Real-time collaboration is absent, so teams bounce off it.
  • Evernote: the clipper and search are still best-in-class, but the free tier (50 notes) is too tight to evaluate, and the paid tier adds little new year over year.

The skeptic's bottom line on "AI" add-ons

All three now sell AI as a paid extra (Notion AI, Evernote AI, Obsidian via local-model plugins). Do the math: Notion AI runs ~$8–10/member/month on top — that can double a small team's bill for a summarizer you could run by pasting text into any chatbot. Obsidian's local-LLM route costs $0 if you already have the hardware. Pay for AI here only after proving the built-in features fell short.

Frequently asked questions

Which is free forever?

Obsidian's core app is free with no note cap. Notion Free is generous but cloud-only and block-limited. Evernote Free is capped at 50 notes — too small to live in.

Can I switch later without losing notes?

Obsidian's Markdown exports anywhere. Notion and Evernote exports are workable but lossy on formatting and databases. If portability matters, Obsidian wins by design.

Is Evernote still worth paying for?

Only if the web clipper and OCR search are central to your workflow. At ~$130/yr, a free clipper plus a notes app covers most people. The paid tier's yearly new features have been thin.

Best for a two-person team?

Notion, if you co-edit live — at ~$96/member/yr for two people that's under $200/year. Obsidian can't co-edit, and Evernote's sharing is notes-only, not true collaboration.

Verdict: Pick by role, not by feature count. Notion for teams that live in shared databases; Obsidian for individuals who value ownership, offline, and portability (and can cost $0); Evernote only if clipping and search are your whole workflow. The honest caveat: the free tiers are not equal — Evernote's 50-note cap is a trial, while Obsidian's free app is the real product. Decide after a week in the one that matches your role.
