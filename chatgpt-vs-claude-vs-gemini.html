"AI assistant" is three different jobs in one chat box

Call them all "AI assistants" and you've already made the first mistake. ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are not three flavors of the same tool — they're three different products that happen to open with a text field. One is a general-purpose workspace with an app store bolted on. One is a careful writing and analysis engine. One is a multimodal layer glued into your Google account. If you pick by "which is best," you're asking the wrong question. The useful question is: which one is built for the job I actually do?

So instead of crowning a winner, let's interview each one and let them state their case — then lay the arguments side by side.

The case for ChatGPT

What it says about itself: OpenAI positions ChatGPT as the general-purpose default — a chat tool that grew into a platform. As its own materials describe it, the value is in breadth: you can talk, draft, generate images, and wire it into hundreds of third-party apps through GPTs and connectors.

The argument for: If your work is varied — a bit of writing, a bit of coding, the occasional image, a Zapier automation — one subscription covers most of it. The community surface (custom GPTs, shared workflows) is larger than anything the other two offer.

The argument against: That 128K context window is the smallest of the three. If your real task is "read this 300-page PDF and answer questions about it," ChatGPT runs out of room first. And breadth is not depth — it does many things, but the writing craft and the Google integration are each beaten by a specialist below.

The case for Claude

What it says about itself: Anthropic frames Claude around being helpful, honest, and careful — a model tuned for nuanced writing and for saying "I don't know" rather than guessing. Its public positioning leans on reliability over flash.

The argument for: For long documents, editing, and analysis, Claude's 200K window and steadier prose are the practical pick. You can drop an entire manuscript or a 150-page report into one conversation. It is the tool people reach for when the output has to read like a human wrote it.

The argument against: No image generation, and a thinner integration story. If you want a bot that lives inside your calendar and inbox, Claude makes you do more wiring yourself. The app ecosystem is younger than ChatGPT's.

The case for Gemini

What it says about itself: Google describes Gemini as natively multimodal and built into the Workspace world — text, images, audio, and video understood from the ground up, not bolted on.

The argument for: The 1,000,000-token context is in a different league, and if you already live in Gmail, Docs, and Drive, Gemini can read your files and draft replies without you exporting anything. The free tier is the most capable of the three.

The argument against: The deep integration only pays off if you're already a Google-account person. Outside Workspace, its third-party app support is the weakest of the trio, and real-world reports are mixed on consistency for non-Google tasks.

Real scenarios, not feature lists

  • A novelist editing a 120,000-word manuscript: Claude's 200K window fits the whole book in one chat. ChatGPT would need chunking; Gemini would too, but adds little here.
  • An analyst summarizing 40 meeting transcripts in Drive: Gemini reads them where they sit. The others need you to export first.
  • A solo founder juggling support, a landing page, and product shots: ChatGPT's image generation plus connectors cover more of the day in one tab.

Frequently asked questions

Can I just use two of them?

Yes, and many people do. A common split is Claude for writing/analysis and ChatGPT (or Gemini) for everything else. All three have free tiers, so the cost of trying is low — the cost of not trying is sticking with the wrong default for a year.

Which has the longest memory?

On paper, Gemini's 1,000,000-token window is the largest. In daily use, Claude's 200K window is the one most people actually rely on for "put the whole document in." ChatGPT's 128K is fine for most single-doc tasks but is the first to overflow on big uploads.

Is the free tier enough to decide?

For a verdict, yes. Spend a week each on your real tasks — drafting an email, summarizing a file, generating an image — and the fit becomes obvious. Pay only when a limit actually blocks you.

Which is safest with private data?

Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google all publish enterprise data-handling terms, and all three offer business tiers with stronger controls. For maximum control, none of the three beats running a local model — see my AI tools guide before sending sensitive documents to any cloud service.

Verdict: There is no "best" — only the one that matches your loop. Start from the scenario, not the leaderboard: Claude if your work is words and long documents, Gemini if your work lives in Google's apps and needs huge context, ChatGPT if you want one tab that touches everything including images. The one honest caveat: every price and model name above will be stale within a quarter, so re-check the linked pages before you commit.
