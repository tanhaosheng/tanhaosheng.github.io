2 a.m., a smart bulb, and a silent hub

The bulb won't connect. The app says "device offline." You reboot the hub; nothing. This is IoT in its rawest form: a sensor, a radio, and a network that has to agree on how to talk. The reason your bulb is sulking is almost never the bulb — it's that WiFi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, and Z-Wave are different radios with different rules, and whoever set this up mixed them without knowing which does what. So let's put the protocols on a bench and compare them like parts, not marketing.

What the numbers mean in a real house

  • WiFi is the only one here that moves serious data, which is why cameras and smart speakers use it. Its cost is power and congestion — a WiFi bulb draws far more than a Zigbee one and competes with your laptop for airtime.
  • Zigbee and Z-Wave are the quiet workhorses of automation. They sip power and mesh, so a sensor at the far end of the house talks through its neighbors. Z-Wave's sub-GHz band penetrates walls better; Zigbee is cheaper and more common in bulbs.
  • Thread is the newer mesh that speaks IPv6 natively, which is why it underpins Matter — the application-layer standard from the Connectivity Standards Alliance meant to let a device work across Apple, Google, Amazon, and Samsung ecosystems without per-brand hubs.
  • Bluetooth LE is what your phone uses to first-pair a device, then usually hands off to a mesh radio for daily operation.

The security footnote nobody reads

Operators who've been burned (the 2016 Mirai botnet turned hundreds of thousands of default-password IoT devices into a DDoS weapon) are blunt: change default credentials, keep firmware updated, and isolate IoT onto a separate VLAN where possible. The radio choice doesn't fix a device with a hardcoded password.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need Matter to make things work together?

Matter helps devices from different ecosystems interoperate over Thread, WiFi, or Ethernet. If you're starting fresh in 2026, buying Matter-certified gear reduces the hub sprawl that caused the 2 a.m. bulb problem.

Which protocol for battery sensors?

Zigbee, Z-Wave, or Thread — all low-power mesh. WiFi will drain a coin-cell sensor in weeks. Use WiFi only for mains-powered, bandwidth-hungry devices like cameras.

Is Zigbee or Z-Wave better?

Z-Wave penetrates walls better (sub-GHz) and has tighter certification; Zigbee is cheaper, faster, and more ubiquitous in lighting. Either beats WiFi for a sensor mesh.

Can IoT devices work without the cloud?

Many can run locally over their mesh, but cloud dependency varies by vendor. For privacy and resilience, prefer devices that support local control and Matter/Thread.

Verdict: Pick the radio by the device, not the brand. Mains-powered and bandwidth-hungry → WiFi. Battery sensors and lighting → Zigbee/Z-Wave/Thread mesh. Want cross-ecosystem sanity → Matter-over-Thread. And whatever you buy, fix the defaults the botnets exploit. Confirm range and compatibility on the vendor's spec page for your actual walls.
