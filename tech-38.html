The two numbers nobody checks

Smart-home marketing runs on two claims: "save 15% on energy" and "works with everything." Both survive only until you do the arithmetic. This is a teardown, not a buyer's circle. We take the claims apart.

Teardown 1: the "save 15%" math

A smart thermostat controls HVAC. In a typical US home, HVAC is roughly 40–50% of total energy use (heating plus cooling). ENERGY STAR states certified smart thermostats save about 8% on heating and cooling bills on average. Google's own Nest figure is ~10–12% on heating and up to 15% on cooling — and that top number assumes geofencing and schedule learning actually fit your life.

The compound reality: 15% of the HVAC slice, not 15% of the bill. If HVAC is 45% of your bill, a best-case 15% HVAC saving is ~6.75% of the total. Real-world, ENERGY STAR's ~8% of HVAC is the honest anchor. The "15%" headline is the ceiling, not the floor.

Teardown 2: "works with everything"

The promise is Matter — a single standard so any device works with any hub. The spec reality is version fragmentation:

Matter 1.3 (2024) and 1.4 (2025/2026) expanded device types and bridged more legacy gear. But a Matter 1.1 device can't magically gain a 1.4 feature, and not every "Matter-compatible" label means full local control. The claim is directionally true and locally incomplete.

Teardown 3: cloud latency and the failure point

A cloud-dependent automation adds a round trip — often 100–500 ms, sometimes seconds — and a hard dependency: if the vendor's server is down, your "smart" light is dumb. Local-first platforms (Home Assistant, Hubitat) keep logic on-premise. The trade-off is setup complexity for reliability. For security-critical automations (leak sensors, locks), local control is the correct default.

A real automation, in config

Home Assistant YAML — a motion-triggered light that only fires after sunset and cancels on manual off:

automation:
  - alias: "hall light on motion (night only)"
    trigger:
      - platform: state
        entity_id: binary_sensor.hall_motion
        to: "on"
    condition:
      - condition: sun
        after: sunset
    action:
      - service: light.turn_on
        target: { entity_id: light.hall }

What to actually buy

  • Max savings: an ENERGY STAR thermostat (Nest, Ecobee) — expect single-digit % of the total bill.
  • Max interoperability: buy Matter-certified devices; pick a hub that runs local (Home Assistant Green, Homey, Hubitat).
  • Max reliability for safety: local-first hub, Zigbee/Z-Wave sensors, no cloud dependency for locks/leaks.
  • Min effort: a single-vendor cloud ecosystem (Alexa + branded devices) — accept the latency and lock-in.

Frequently asked questions

Is Matter finally "done"?

Functionally yes for the common device classes, but the spec keeps growing (1.3, 1.4 added device types and bridging). Buy Matter-certified and you're future-proofed better than any single vendor's older protocol — just don't expect a 2023 device to gain 2026 features.

Do smart devices spy on me?

Cloud-connected devices can log usage; local-first platforms minimize that. The privacy question is really a "where does my data go" question — read the hub's data policy, and prefer on-premise logic for anything sensitive.

Will a smart thermostat pay for itself?

At ~8% HVAC savings, a $130 thermostat offsets itself against energy prices over a few years in a high-use home; slower in a mild climate. The 15% marketing case is the best, not the typical, return.

Zigbee or Z-Wave in 2026?

Both remain valid for legacy gear. For new buys, Matter-over-Thread is the forward path; Z-Wave's sub-GHz band still wins for wall penetration in dense buildings. Match the band to your walls, not the logo.
