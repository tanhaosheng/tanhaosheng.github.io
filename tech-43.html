From OnLive to your browser

Cloud gaming is not new. OnLive demoed it in 2010, and it flopped — the networks weren't ready. Fifteen years later the pitch is identical ("no console, just stream") but the fiber finally is. That hasn't changed the one truth the ads leave out: you are not buying games, you are renting the right to play them on someone else's machine, and that right ends the month you stop paying.

The bandwidth tax

Streaming is not free on your connection. Figures below are vendor-stated requirements:

  • 1080p60: roughly 15–25 Mbps sustained, ~4–7 GB per hour.
  • 4K: roughly 35–50 Mbps, ~15–20 GB per hour.

Do that math against a 1.2 TB monthly data cap and 4K cloud gaming burns through it in well under 60 hours. If your ISP meters data, "no console" quietly becomes "higher internet bill." Wired Ethernet or Wi-Fi 6/7 is the floor; Wi-Fi on a crowded apartment channel will blame the service for your own signal.

The provocation: you own nothing

The single biggest gap between cloud gaming and a console is ownership. Buy a disc or even a downloaded license and it's yours while the store exists. With Xbox Cloud, Luna, and PS Plus Premium, the moment the subscription lapses, the library is gone — including the 200 hours you put into a save. GeForce NOW is the exception and the honest pick for anyone who already owns games: you bring your Steam/Epic licenses and rent only the GPU. Everyone else is paying a streaming fee for access that can be repriced or pulled with a month's notice.

Pick by your actual situation

  • You own a Steam library and want max quality: GeForce NOW Ultimate (~$19.99/mo) — you keep your games, get 4K120.
  • You want the broadest "just works" catalog: Xbox Cloud via Game Pass Ultimate — best value if you'd subscribe anyway for console/PC Game Pass.
  • Prime member, light player: Luna's included rotation costs $0 extra; Luna+ at $9.99 is the cheapest paid tier.
  • PlayStation fan, already paying Premium: the streaming is a bonus you've funded already — don't double-count it.
  • Poor or metered internet: none of these. Buy a used console; the bandwidth math is against you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need fast internet?

Yes for anything above 1080p. Vendors cite 15–25 Mbps for 1080p and 35–50 Mbps for 4K. Latency under ~30ms keeps controls responsive; wired is best. On a metered plan, watch the data use.

Can I keep my saves if I cancel?

With Xbox Cloud, Luna, and PS Plus Premium, no — the catalog is rented. With GeForce NOW, your saves live in the games you own on Steam/Epic, so they persist. Read that difference before committing hundreds of hours.

Is GeForce NOW free worth trying?

The free tier caps sessions at one hour and queues at peak. Fine to test whether your connection holds up; not fine for a long session. Upgrade only if the connection proves stable.

Which is cheapest per hour?

Luna+ at heavy play (~$0.10/hr at 100 hrs/mo) is lowest, but its catalog is smaller. Xbox Cloud and PS Plus sit near $0.17–0.18/hr at heavy use. Light players pay over $1/hr on every tier.

Verdict: Cloud gaming is a streaming subscription, not a console replacement, and the per-hour math only favors it for heavy players with good, unmetered internet. GeForce NOW is the standout for anyone who already owns games; Xbox Cloud is the broadest catalog for the Game Pass crowd. The honest caveat: you own none of the library on the big three, so never sink hours into a save you can't export, and check your data cap before you stream 4K.
