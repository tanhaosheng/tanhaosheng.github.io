How this was tested: This was not a lab benchmark. A sales startup needed a 12-slide pitch deck for a client meeting in one afternoon. I gave them a one-pager summary and asked them to try four AI presentation builders—Gamma, Tome, Beautiful.ai, and Pitch AI—with no training. They used each tool to produce a version of the deck, took notes on the process, and shared the final outputs. What follows is the unedited story of that afternoon, including where each tool helped and where it slowed them down.

The brief: one page, 12 slides, 4 hours

The input was a one-pager: product overview, target market, pricing model, and three customer success quotes. The requirement was a clean, professional deck they could present that afternoon—no frills, just clear messaging and visual support. The team had never used any of these tools before. That's the real-world condition: no training, no preset templates, just a blank canvas and a prompt.

Gamma: the speed leader

Gamma was the first they tried. They pasted the one-pager into the prompt box and clicked Generate. In under 90 seconds, Gamma produced a 12-slide deck with a consistent color scheme, readable fonts, and images that actually matched the content. The output needed minor tweaks—swap out a generic photo for a product screenshot, adjust one slide's headline—but the structure was already there.

Where it frustrated them: Gamma's customization is limited to the built-in themes. When they wanted a non-standard slide transition, Gamma didn't support it. They spent 15 minutes trying to force a design that the tool doesn't easily accommodate, then gave up and went back to the default. Speed wins, but at the cost of flexibility.

Tome: the storyteller

Tome took the same input and produced a deck that felt more like a narrative than a presentation. The slide transitions were cinematic, the pacing was deliberate, and the tone matched a startup's brand voice. But—here's the catch—they had to guide Tome more actively. Tome asked clarifying questions about the tone, the target audience, and which stories to emphasize. The team spent 20 minutes answering those questions before getting a first draft.

Where it impressed them: Once the draft was there, Tome's storytelling structure made the deck feel more cohesive than Gamma's. The sales team liked how Tome grouped related points into thematic sections rather than one point per slide. It took longer to get there, but the final output felt more intentional.

Beautiful.ai: the design police

Beautiful.ai approached the task differently. It didn't generate a full deck from a single prompt. Instead, it offered a library of slide templates and asked the user to select which ones they wanted, then fill in the content. The AI then automatically adjusted layout, spacing, and visual hierarchy to fit the template.

Where it shined: The design quality was impeccable. Every slide looked professionally designed, with consistent alignment and spacing. The team appreciated that Beautiful.ai enforced design rules they didn't know they needed. But—big but—they spent 30 minutes selecting and arranging templates before even starting to write content. For a time crunch, that overhead was too much.

Pitch AI: the educator's choice

Pitch AI was the fourth they tried, primarily because their client was an educational institution. Pitch AI produced a deck with a clean, academic-friendly design, but the team found the prompt-to-output process less intuitive than Gamma or Tome. They had to be more explicit about the tone and structure, and the first draft needed more editing.

Where it fit: For educational or training contexts, Pitch AI's output was clear and readable. But for a sales pitch, it felt a bit too formal. The team liked the customization options but felt the learning curve was steeper than the others for a one-off deck.

When to use each (based on this afternoon's test)

  • Choose Gamma if: You need a deck fast, you have limited design time, and you want a good-enough result that you can polish manually. Best for sales pitches, investor updates, quick client decks.
  • Choose Tome if: Your deck needs a narrative arc, you want the tool to help structure your story, and you're willing to spend a bit more time guiding the AI. Best for brand storytelling, product launches, company overviews.
  • Choose Beautiful.ai if: Design quality is your #1 priority, you have time to select and arrange templates, and you want the AI to handle the layout polish. Best for marketing materials, investor decks, client presentations where design perception matters.
  • Choose Pitch AI if: You're in education, training, or academic contexts, and you need clear, readable slides with an appropriate tone. Best for training decks, educational materials, academic presentations.

What we learned (the unplanned takeaways)

  1. Speed ≠ quality. Gamma was fastest, but Tome's deck felt more polished after minor editing. The time spent guiding Tome paid off in the final output.
  2. Template freedom is a double-edged sword. Beautiful.ai's design enforcement was helpful but also restrictive when they wanted to break the template.
  3. AI isn't a replacement for direction. All four tools needed human input—the better the input, the better the output. A vague prompt gave generic slides; a focused prompt gave useful ones.
  4. Export matters. Gamma and Tome exported to PDF and Google Slides easily. Beautiful.ai's export was more limited. Pitch AI's export worked but required additional formatting steps.
Our pick: For most business use cases—sales decks, investor pitches, client presentations—Gamma is the fastest win if time is tight. If you have time to guide it and want a more narrative deck, Tome is worth the extra effort. Beautiful.ai is the design winner when you have time to work with templates. Pitch AI fits education-specific needs. None is a magic bullet—they all need your direction.
