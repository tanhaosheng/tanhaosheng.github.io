5G Explained in 2026: A Decision Tree, Not a Sales Pitch

📶 5G

How this was compiled: This explainer is built from 3GPP release documentation, GSMA material, and public carrier specifications — not from field tests I ran. Coverage and speeds vary by location; the linked sources are authoritative.

The bold claim

5G is the most oversold and least understood network upgrade of the decade — and most people are using it wrong because the word "5G" hides two completely different technologies. If you buy a phone for "5G speed" without knowing which kind, you may have paid a premium for a badge.

Two radios wearing one name

5G is not one thing. It spans two frequency bands with opposite trade-offs:

PropertySub-6 GHz (low/mid-band)mmWave (24–47 GHz)
CoverageBroad; penetrates buildingsTiny; needs line-of-sight
Real-world speed~100–400 Mbps typical1–4 Gbps under ideal conditions
Latency~10–20 msUnder 10 ms
Where it livesEverywhere 5G existsStadiums, venues, dense urban blocks
Speed vs 4G~2–3× faster10–50× faster, rarely reachable

The trick: carriers advertise mmWave's peak numbers while most users spend their time on sub-6, which is good but not revolutionary. Both are "5G."

The decision tree

Pick your branch:

  • Do you live in a dense city with mmWave buildout? → You might see gigabit speeds at a street corner or arena. Don't assume it follows you indoors.
  • Do you live in suburb or rural area? → You're almost certainly on sub-6, which may be only modestly faster than 4G. Check your carrier's coverage map before believing any "5G" claim.
  • Are you buying a new phone in 2026? → Any current flagship does sub-6. mmWave support is spotty and mostly on specific US-market models — irrelevant unless you're near a mmWave zone daily.
  • Do you tether a laptop or run Fixed Wireless Access at home? → Sub-6 FWA can replace cable in some areas; verify the tower distance, because range still matters.
  • Do you need low latency for cloud gaming or AR? → Sub-6's 10–20 ms is fine for most; true sub-10 ms needs mmWave you probably can't reach.

What's under the hood

Both bands share the clever plumbing that makes 5G better than 4G LTE:

  • Massive MIMO — base stations use many antennas to serve many users at once instead of one beam at a time.
  • Beamforming — signals are steered toward your device rather than broadcast wastefully.
  • Network slicing — one physical network carved into virtual slices with different priorities (e.g., a guaranteed-low-latency slice for emergency services).

The 2026 wrinkle: 5G Advanced

3GPP Release 18 — branded "5G Advanced" — is rolling out through 2026. It adds AI-driven network optimization, sharper positioning, and RedCap (Reduced Capability) devices that give IoT sensors 5G without a flagship-phone radio's cost and power draw. Release 19 work is already underway. None of this changes the sub-6-vs-mmWave decision above; it makes the network smarter around it.

Buying advice without the fluff

  • Don't pay extra for "5G" alone — it's standard now.
  • Don't chase mmWave unless you've confirmed coverage at the places you actually stand.
  • If your carrier's sub-6 map is thin where you live, 4G may serve you just as well today.
  • For home internet, test Fixed Wireless Access for a month before canceling cable.

Frequently asked questions

Is 5G dangerous to health?

Regulators (FCC, ICNIRP) have reviewed the radio-frequency exposure of 5G and found it within existing safety limits; mmWave in particular is absorbed by skin and doesn't penetrate deeply. This is the consensus of the health agencies that set exposure standards, not a settled end to public debate — but no credible body has linked compliant 5G to harm.

Will 5G replace my Wi-Fi?

No. 5G and Wi-Fi are complementary. 5G covers you outdoors and on the move; Wi-Fi is cheaper and higher-capacity at home. Your phone uses both all day without you noticing.

Why is my 5G sometimes slower than 4G?

Coverage, congestion, and band matter more than the badge. A crowded sub-6 5G cell can underperform a clean 4G signal. The network name doesn't guarantee the experience.

What is 5G Advanced, in one line?

Release 18's smarter, AI-optimized 5G with better positioning and lightweight IoT (RedCap) support — an evolution of the same two bands, not a third kind of 5G.

Verdict: 5G in 2026 is genuinely better than 4G, but "better" mostly means solid sub-6 coverage, not the gigabit mmWave headline number. Use the decision tree above: most buyers need only standard sub-6, and mmWave is a niche perk you should verify on a coverage map before valuing. The caveat — real speeds and latency depend entirely on your carrier, your location, and which band you're actually on; the linked specs are the ceiling, not your lived experience.
