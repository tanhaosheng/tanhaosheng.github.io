Strength Training for Muscle and Bone Health

Strength training is not just about building muscle. It improves bone density, boosts metabolism, enhances joint function, and supports healthy aging.

Key Benefits of Resistance Training

Regular strength training increases lean muscle mass, which raises your resting metabolic rate. It strengthens connective tissues, improves posture, and reduces the risk of osteoporosis. It also helps manage blood sugar levels.

Compound vs. Isolation Exercises

Compound exercises like squats, deadlifts, bench presses, and rows work multiple muscle groups simultaneously and provide the most functional strength. Isolation exercises target specific muscles and are useful for addressing imbalances.

Getting Started with Weights

Begin with bodyweight exercises such as push-ups, squats, and lunges. Progress to resistance bands, dumbbells, or barbells as you build confidence. Focus on proper form before increasing weight to prevent injury.

Designing a Training Program

Aim for two to three strength sessions per week, allowing at least 48 hours between sessions for muscle recovery. Perform eight to twelve repetitions per set for hypertrophy, and complete two to three sets per exercise.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Lifting too heavy too soon, neglecting warm-up sets, skipping rest days, and poor form are common pitfalls. Progress gradually, listen to your body, and consider working with a trainer when starting out.
