Notion vs Obsidian vs Evernote: Full Comparison 2026

Published on AI Tool Stack | Tech

Three apps dominate the personal productivity and knowledge management space: Notion (all-in-one workspace), Obsidian (local-first markdown), and Evernote (the OG note-taking giant). Each has over 100 million users, but they serve fundamentally different workflows. Here's how they stack up in 2026 after three months of testing each one daily.

Notion — The All-in-One Workspace

Notion is no longer just a note-taking app. It's a database engine, wiki, project board, CRM, spreadsheet, and document editor wrapped into one. Its block-based editor and relational databases let you build complex workspaces from scratch — or use thousands of community templates.

Strengths: Unmatched flexibility. If you can imagine it, you can build it in Notion. Real-time collaboration is genuinely good. The free plan covers 90% of individual users. Notion AI ($10/month add-on) can summarize pages, rewrite content, generate tables, and translate languages.

Weaknesses: Cloud-only means offline access is still spotty despite beta improvements. Privacy-conscious users don't like their data sitting exclusively on Notion servers. Performance degrades noticeably with large workspaces (10K+ blocks).

Obsidian — The Local-First Knowledge Graph

Obsidian stores everything as plain Markdown files on your device. This means complete ownership, infinite customizability, and a plugin ecosystem that rivals entire apps. The "graph view" shows connections between notes visually — making it perfect for researchers, academics, and anyone building a "second brain."

Strengths: Your data is yours — always, everywhere, forever. Offline-first by design, lightning-fast. Community plugins (Dataview, Templater, QuickAdd, Tasks) can transform Obsidian into virtually any kind of knowledge management system. Can connect to local LLMs (Ollama, LM Studio) for private AI.

Weaknesses: No native real-time collaboration. Sync across devices costs $8/month per user (Unison). Steep learning curve for power users who want to configure plugins and themes. No built-in databases — everything requires plugin magic.

Evernote — The Reliable Veteran

Evernote invented the modern note-taking app category and has been refining it ever since. In 2026, it's positioned as a streamlined, reliable tool for individual note capture and retrieval — less about building complex systems, more about writing things down and finding them again.

Strengths: Best-in-class web clipper. Outstanding search (OCR on images and PDFs). Simple, familiar interface. Evernote AI can summarize notes, suggest tags, rewrite text, and answer questions about your notebooks.

Weaknesses: Free plan limits you to 50 notes and 1 notebook. The $8-15/mo tiers haven't introduced killer new features in years. Data export is possible but clunky. Feels like a feature-frozen product compared to Notion and Obsidian's rapid evolution.

Which Should You Choose?

  • You run a team or need shared workspaces? Notion. Nothing else comes close for collaboration.
  • You value data privacy and offline access above all? Obsidian. Your data lives on your machine, period.
  • You want something simple that works? Evernote. Less configurability, but also less to break.
  • You're an academic or researcher building a knowledge base? Obsidian + Dataview plugin (or Notion if collaborating).
  • You just want to clip articles and read them later? Evernote's web clipper is still the king.
