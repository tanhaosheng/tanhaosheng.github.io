DeepSeek AI: Complete Beginner's Guide 2026

How to Use China's Most Powerful AI Model — From Zero to Pro

If you follow AI news at all, you have heard the name: DeepSeek. In late 2024 and throughout 2025, this Chinese AI lab released a series of models that sent shockwaves through Silicon Valley. A reasoning model that matched OpenAI's o1 at a fraction of the cost. An open-source model that rivaled GPT-4. API pricing so cheap it forced competitors to slash their own rates. By mid-2026, DeepSeek is not just a curiosity — it is a genuine contender for the best all-around AI assistant available, and it is completely free to use on the web.

This guide covers everything you need to know: what DeepSeek is, how it works, how to access it, and how to use it for real tasks like writing, coding, and research. Whether you are a complete beginner or a seasoned AI user curious about the Chinese AI ecosystem, this is the place to start.

What Is DeepSeek?

DeepSeek is an AI research company based in China, founded by Liang Wenfeng. It started as a quantitative hedge fund's side project (High-Flyer Quant) before spinning off into its own AI lab. The company has released a series of increasingly powerful large language models that have drawn global attention for their performance, efficiency, and extremely low cost.

The key milestones in DeepSeek's rapid rise:

  • DeepSeek-V2 (mid-2024) — The first major model that put DeepSeek on the map. It introduced Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA), a novel architecture that dramatically reduced memory usage and inference cost.
  • DeepSeek-V3 (December 2024) — A 671-billion parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model. It matched or exceeded GPT-4 on several benchmarks while costing a fraction to train and run. The AI world took notice.
  • DeepSeek-R1 (January 2025) — The bombshell. A reasoning model that performed on par with OpenAI's o1, achieved through pure reinforcement learning rather than supervised fine-tuning. Its "thinking" process was published openly, and the model was released as open source. This was the moment DeepSeek became a household name.
  • DeepSeek-R1-0528 and DeepSeek-V3-0324 (2025) — Updated versions that further improved reasoning, coding, and multilingual performance.

As of July 2026, DeepSeek's latest models consistently rank among the top performers on the LMSYS Chatbot Arena leaderboard, competing directly with GPT-4.5, Claude 4 Opus, and Gemini 2.5 Pro.

Why it matters: DeepSeek proved that frontier AI does not require billions of dollars in compute. By optimizing its Mixture-of-Experts architecture, DeepSeek trained V3 for under $6 million — a fraction of the estimated cost of comparable US models. This has fundamentally changed the economics of AI development.

How to Access DeepSeek

Getting started with DeepSeek is simple. There are three main ways to use it.

Web app (recommended for beginners). Go to chat.deepseek.com in your browser. You will see a welcome screen with options to log in or sign up. Click "Sign Up" and register with your email address or Google account. No phone verification is required for most regions. Once logged in, you can start chatting immediately — no credit card needed, no payment setup.
Mobile app. DeepSeek has official apps on both the Apple App Store and Google Play Store. Search for "DeepSeek" and look for the app by "深度求索" (DeepSeek's Chinese name). The mobile app supports voice input, image upload, and file attachments. It works well on both phones and tablets.
API (for developers). DeepSeek offers a paid API at platform.deepseek.com. You create an account, generate an API key, and top up your balance. The API is OpenAI-compatible, meaning any code written for the OpenAI API can be adapted to use DeepSeek by changing the base URL and model name. Pricing is significantly cheaper than OpenAI — typically 10x to 20x less expensive.
Note for US users: DeepSeek's web app and API remain accessible from the United States as of July 2026, though the legal landscape has been uncertain. The company has taken steps to comply with international regulations. If you encounter access issues, using a VPN may help. DeepSeek states that user data from outside China is stored on servers in Singapore.

Key Features of DeepSeek

DeepSeek packs several standout features that set it apart from other AI assistants. Here is what you get out of the box.

1 Million Token Context

DeepSeek supports a context window of up to 1 million tokens — roughly the length of all three volumes of "The Three-Body Problem" trilogy combined. You can paste entire books, codebases, or research papers into the chat and DeepSeek will understand and reason about them in full.

Reasoning Mode (R1)

The R1 reasoning model shows its step-by-step "thinking" process before delivering the final answer. This makes it exceptionally good at math, logic puzzles, complex coding tasks, and multi-step problem solving. You can watch how the model arrives at its conclusions.

File Upload & OCR

DeepSeek accepts image uploads (JPG, PNG), PDFs, Word documents, Excel files, PowerPoint presentations, and text files. It can extract and read text from images using built-in OCR, making it useful for digitizing printed documents or analyzing screenshots.

Web Search Integration

DeepSeek can search the internet for current information. On the web app, toggle the "Web Search" button before asking a question. The model will browse relevant pages and incorporate up-to-date information into its response. This is essential for news, stock prices, and facts beyond its training cutoff.

Voice Input

The mobile app supports voice input on both iOS and Android. You can speak your questions instead of typing them, making it convenient for hands-free use while cooking, commuting, or exercising.

Free Tier

The web and mobile apps are completely free to use with no usage limits for standard chat. DeepSeek does not cap messages or charge per query on its consumer app. This is extraordinary value compared to ChatGPT (which limits free usage) or Claude (which restricts free tier access in many regions).

How to Use DeepSeek: Step-by-Step Guide

Here is how to get the most out of DeepSeek for three common tasks: writing, coding, and research.

Using DeepSeek for Writing

Open DeepSeek in your browser at chat.deepseek.com and log in. Enable "Web Search" if your writing task requires current facts or references.
Craft a specific prompt. Instead of "Write a blog post," try something like: "Write a 1000-word blog post about the benefits of electric vehicles for first-time car buyers. Use a conversational tone, include a comparison table of the top 3 EVs in 2026, and cite the latest range and price data."
Review and refine. Read DeepSeek's response. Ask for revisions: "Make the introduction more engaging," "Add a section about charging infrastructure," or "Shorten this to 600 words." DeepSeek remembers the context within the same conversation.
Export your work. Copy the text, or use DeepSeek's share feature to generate a link to the conversation. You can also paste it into your preferred editor for final formatting.

Using DeepSeek for Coding

Enable DeepSeek's reasoning mode. For coding tasks, use the R1 model (selectable from the model dropdown). R1's step-by-step reasoning produces more reliable code and better debugging.
Describe the problem thoroughly. Provide context: "Write a Python script that reads a CSV file of customer orders, calculates total revenue by region, and outputs a bar chart as a PNG file. The CSV has columns: order_id, region, amount, date." The more detail you give, the better the output.
Review the code. DeepSeek will generate code along with an explanation. Check for correctness, and test it in your environment. You can paste error messages back into DeepSeek for debugging assistance.
Iterate with follow-ups. Ask for improvements: "Add error handling," "Convert this to use async/await," "Write unit tests for the main function." DeepSeek can handle complex multi-file projects when guided step by step.

Using DeepSeek for Research

Upload your source material. DeepSeek's 1M context window means you can upload entire research papers, PDFs, or long reports. Click the attachment icon and select your file.
Ask specific questions. "Summarize this paper in 5 bullet points," "What methodology did the authors use?" "What are the limitations of this study?" DeepSeek will reference specific parts of the uploaded document.
Enable web search for verification. For topics that require up-to-date data, toggle web search. DeepSeek can cross-reference your uploaded documents with current online information.
Synthesize findings. Ask DeepSeek to combine insights from multiple sources: "Based on the attached research paper and the latest news, what is the current state of solid-state battery development?"

DeepSeek R1 Reasoning Model Explained

DeepSeek R1 is arguably the most important AI release of 2025. It is a "reasoning model" — a type of AI that explicitly works through problems step by step before arriving at an answer, similar to how a human might solve a complex puzzle on a whiteboard.

What made R1 revolutionary was not just its performance (it matched OpenAI's o1 on math and coding benchmarks), but how it was created. DeepSeek used pure reinforcement learning — the model taught itself to reason through trial and error, without any human-annotated reasoning examples. It spontaneously developed behaviors like self-verification (double-checking its own work) and chain-of-thought thinking entirely on its own.

When you use R1 on DeepSeek's web app, you see its "thinking" process in real time. The model shows you its internal monologue — re-reading the question, exploring different approaches, catching its own mistakes, and finally settling on an answer. This transparency is unique to DeepSeek and has made it a favorite among researchers, educators, and anyone who wants to understand why an AI gave a particular answer.

Pro tip: To use R1 on the DeepSeek web app, look for the model selector at the top of the chat screen. Switch from "DeepSeek" (the standard V3 model) to "DeepSeek-R1" for tasks that require deep reasoning — math, logic puzzles, complex coding, or any problem where step-by-step thinking matters. For general conversation and creative writing, the standard V3 model is faster and works perfectly well.

Strengths and Limitations

DeepSeek is an impressive tool, but it is important to understand both what it does well and where it falls short.

Strengths

  • Extraordinary value. The free consumer tier is genuinely unlimited with no hidden caps. The API is 10-20x cheaper than US competitors. For budget-conscious users and startups, DeepSeek is the clear winner.
  • 1M context window. Only Gemini 2.5 Pro matches this in the consumer market. ChatGPT and Claude offer far smaller context windows (128K and 200K respectively).
  • Open-source models. DeepSeek releases its model weights publicly, allowing researchers and companies to run the models on their own hardware. This has spawned a vibrant ecosystem of fine-tuned variants.
  • Transparent reasoning. R1's visible thinking process is unmatched for educational purposes and debugging.
  • Strong coding performance. DeepSeek consistently scores at or near the top on coding benchmarks like SWE-Bench and HumanEval.

Limitations

  • Multimodal capabilities are limited. DeepSeek can read text from images via OCR but does not have true visual understanding like GPT-4.5 or Claude. It cannot analyze a photo's composition, identify objects in an image, or generate images.
  • Chinese-language bias. DeepSeek performs best in Chinese and English. For other languages, quality may be inconsistent.
  • Geopolitical uncertainty. US export restrictions on advanced chips to China could affect DeepSeek's ability to train future models. The regulatory environment is unpredictable.
  • No voice output. DeepSeek supports voice input but does not have a text-to-speech voice response feature (unlike ChatGPT's voice mode).
  • Occasional censorship. Like all Chinese AI models, DeepSeek may refuse to answer certain politically sensitive questions, particularly those involving Chinese government topics.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is DeepSeek safe to use?

Yes, DeepSeek is safe for everyday use. The company states that conversations are encrypted and user data from outside China is stored on servers in Singapore. However, as with any AI service, you should avoid sharing sensitive personal information like passwords, ID numbers, or financial details. DeepSeek's privacy policy has been reviewed by international security researchers and is comparable to other major AI services.

Can I use DeepSeek from the United States or Europe?

Yes, DeepSeek is accessible from most countries including the United States and European Union. The web app and mobile apps are available globally. There have been some temporary access disruptions due to geopolitical events, but as of July 2026, DeepSeek remains fully accessible from Western countries. US users should be aware that the legal landscape around Chinese AI services continues to evolve.

Is DeepSeek really free? What is the catch?

DeepSeek's consumer app is genuinely free with no usage limits. There is no catch. The company makes money from its API services (used by businesses and developers) and likely views the free consumer app as both a showcase for its technology and a data source for ongoing model improvement. The economics work because DeepSeek's Mixture-of-Experts architecture is extremely efficient to run.

How does DeepSeek compare to ChatGPT for coding?

DeepSeek R1 is widely considered to be on par with or slightly ahead of ChatGPT (GPT-4.5) for coding tasks, particularly for algorithmic problems and debugging. On the SWE-Bench benchmark (which tests real-world software engineering tasks), DeepSeek models have consistently ranked among the top performers. ChatGPT has an advantage in broader software development contexts due to its plugins and integration ecosystem.

Does DeepSeek have a mobile app?

Yes, DeepSeek has official mobile apps for both iOS and Android. You can find them by searching for "DeepSeek" in the Apple App Store or Google Play Store. The app supports voice input, file uploads, and conversation syncing across devices. It is the same free service as the web app with a mobile-optimized interface.
