Resume Writing: Stand Out to Employers

Your resume is often your first impression with a potential employer. A well-crafted resume highlights your strengths and gets you past screening systems.

Formatting for Readability

Use a clean, professional layout with consistent fonts and spacing. Keep your resume to one page for most positions. Use bullet points for achievements rather than paragraphs. Save as PDF unless instructed otherwise to preserve formatting.

Tailor for Each Application

Generic resumes rarely succeed. Analyze the job description and match keywords, skills, and experiences. Customize your professional summary for each role. Highlight the achievements most relevant to the specific position you are targeting.

Quantify Your Achievements

Use numbers to demonstrate impact. Instead of managed a team, write led a team of 12 and increased productivity by 25 percent within six months. Specific metrics make your contributions concrete and memorable to hiring managers.

Navigating Applicant Tracking Systems

Most companies use ATS software to screen resumes. Use standard section headings, include keywords from the job description, and avoid complex formatting like tables or graphics. Simple, text-based resumes parse most reliably.

Common Resume Mistakes

Typos, inconsistent formatting, missing contact information, and including irrelevant experience are common errors. List accomplishments, not just responsibilities. Avoid cliches like hardworking and team player without evidence. Proofread multiple times.
