Sleep Hygiene: Habits for Better Rest

Sleep hygiene refers to the habits and practices that promote consistent, uninterrupted sleep. Good sleep hygiene is essential for physical health, cognitive function, and emotional well-being.

Consistency Is Key

Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, including weekends. A consistent schedule reinforces your body's circadian rhythm, making it easier to fall asleep and wake naturally.

Optimize Your Sleep Environment

Keep your bedroom cool, dark, and quiet. Invest in a comfortable mattress and pillows. Remove electronic devices or use blue light filters. Consider blackout curtains and white noise machines if light or noise is an issue.

Create a Wind-Down Routine

Signal to your body that sleep is coming by engaging in relaxing activities for 30 to 60 minutes before bed. Read a physical book, take a warm bath, practice gentle stretching, or listen to calming music. Avoid screens during this time.

Daytime Habits That Affect Sleep

Expose yourself to natural light in the morning, exercise regularly but not too close to bedtime, limit caffeine after midday, and avoid heavy meals before sleep. Napping late in the day can disrupt nighttime sleep.

What to Do When You Cannot Sleep

If you are lying awake for more than 20 minutes, get out of bed and do something calming in dim light until you feel sleepy. Avoid clock-watching, which increases anxiety. Return to bed only when drowsy.
