Mindfulness: Living in the Present Moment

Mindfulness is the practice of paying attention to the present moment with openness and curiosity. It is a simple concept with profound effects on well-being.

The Science of Mindfulness

Research shows that regular mindfulness practice changes brain structure and function. It reduces activity in the default mode network associated with mind-wandering and rumination, while strengthening areas involved in attention and emotional regulation.

Mindful Breathing

The breath is an anchor to the present moment. Take a few minutes to simply notice the sensation of breathing the air entering your nostrils, the rise and fall of your chest. When thoughts arise, return to the breath without judgment.

Mindful Eating

Instead of eating on autopilot, engage all your senses. Notice the colors, textures, and aromas of your food. Chew slowly and savor each bite. Mindful eating improves digestion, prevents overeating, and increases enjoyment of meals.

Mindfulness in Daily Activities

You can practice mindfulness while walking, washing dishes, or brushing your teeth. Bring full attention to the sensations and movements involved. These micro-practices accumulate into a more centered, peaceful state of being.

Overcoming Mindfulness Barriers

Common obstacles include thinking you are doing it wrong, feeling too busy, or becoming frustrated when the mind wanders. Remember that noticing distraction and returning to focus is the essence of the practice.
