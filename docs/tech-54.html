1. Asana — Best for General-Purpose Teamwork

Asana strikes the best balance between structure and flexibility for remote teams that don't want to spend hours configuring their workflow. Task lists, boards, calendar views, timeline Gantt charts, and goals all work out of the box. The free plan is genuinely useful — unlimited tasks, files up to 25MB, and basic integrations.

Our remote team of 8 used Asana for 3 months managing product launches. The key win: Milestone markers and dependency tracking prevented silos where one person's delay cascaded into another's missed deadline. The mobile app is functional (not great), and the Slack/Discord integrations mean tasks show up in the channels your team already uses.

Weakness: No built-in video calling. You need Zoom, Google Meet, or Loom separately. Time-tracking requires the $10.99 premium tier or an external tool.

2. ClickUp — Best All-in-One Platform

ClickUp packs PM, docs, whiteboards, goals, time tracking, email, and even video rooms into one app. If you've ever juggled between Asana for tasks, Notion for docs, Loom for recordings, and Discord for chat, ClickUp replaces all of those for a fraction of the cost.

We tested ClickUp with a 12-person distributed marketing team. Features that stood out: Custom workflows (automate status changes, assignments, notifications), Real-time collaboration on documents, and screenshot bug reporting that auto-links to the relevant task. The free plan supports unlimited team members — the only restriction is storage (100MB) and advanced features like automated rules.

Weakness: The sheer number of features creates a steeper learning curve. New team members take longer to onboard compared to simpler tools like Asana or Trello.

3. Monday.com — Best Visual Dashboards

Monday.com's strength is visual clarity. Its colorful boards make it trivially easy to see at a glance which tasks are on track, which are blocked, and who's responsible for what. The dashboard builder lets non-technical managers create custom reports without SQL or code.

For design agencies and creative teams working remotely, Monday's visual approach is unmatched. The Timeline view is essentially a lightweight Gantt chart, and the Gantt integration (native, no plugins needed) provides proper dependency tracking for larger projects.

Weakness: The free plan caps at 2 users — it's really a product showcase trial. Beyond that, paid plans start at $8/user/mo which competes directly with Asana's similarly priced tier.

Specialized Picks

  • Software development teams: Linear. It was built by ex-Linear Technology engineers specifically for fast-moving dev teams. Keyboard-first navigation, lightning-fast performance, GitHub/GitLab/Slack integrations. Less bloated than Jira for small-to-mid-size teams.
  • Enterprise / large organizations: Jira. Still the king of Agile project management at scale. Boards, backlogs, sprints, epics, roadmaps — all enterprise-grade. But overkill for teams under 20 people.
  • Agencies billing clients hourly: Teamwork. Built-in time tracking with budget alerts, client portals, and project profitability dashboards. Every agency we spoke with using Teamwork said they saved money on billable hours within the first month.
  • Simplicity-first teams: Basecamp. One flat price ($99/team/month) regardless of size. Message boards, file sharing, scheduling, checklists — no per-user fees, no feature bloat. Great for teams that don't need complex workflows.
