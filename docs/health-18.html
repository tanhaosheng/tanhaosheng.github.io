Circadian Rhythm and Your Body Clock

Your circadian rhythm is an internal 24-hour clock that regulates sleep, hormone release, body temperature, and metabolism. It influences nearly every system in your body.

How the Body Clock Works

Light is the primary cue that synchronizes your circadian rhythm. Specialized cells in your eyes detect light and signal the brain's suprachiasmatic nucleus, which in turn regulates melatonin production. Darkness triggers melatonin release, promoting sleep.

What Disrupts Circadian Rhythms

Shift work, jet lag, late-night screen exposure, irregular sleep schedules, and artificial light at night can all throw your body clock off balance. Chronic disruption is linked to obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and mood disorders.

The Role of Melatonin

Melatonin is the hormone that signals your body to prepare for sleep. Its production increases in the evening and decreases in the morning. Melatonin supplements can help with jet lag or shift work but should be used judiciously.

Aligning Your Rhythm Naturally

Get bright light exposure in the morning, especially sunlight. Dim lights in the evening and avoid screens an hour before bed. Eat meals at consistent times, and exercise earlier in the day to reinforce your natural rhythm.

Circadian Rhythm and Meal Timing

Your digestive system follows a circadian pattern too. Eating late at night can disrupt both sleep and metabolism. Try to finish your last meal at least two to three hours before bedtime for optimal alignment.
